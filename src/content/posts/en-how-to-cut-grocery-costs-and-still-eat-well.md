## Plan the list before you go

1. **Set the meals for the week** – Think about the dishes you’ll prepare and write down only the ingredients you actually need.  
2. **Check what you already have at home** – Open the fridge and pantry; avoid buying items you already have in stock.  
3. **Set limits by category** – Use a rough amount, like “the price of a coffee per day” for dairy, “a minimum‑wage amount” for meat, etc.

**Practical tip:** *Before you leave, open FinMoovi, create a shopping list, and turn on the “real‑time total” mode. The app will show, after each item is checked off, how much of your budget remains.*  

## Use smart receipt capture

When you get home, pull the receipt from the bag and, with a few taps, log it in FinMoovi. The app recognizes the products, groups them into categories (fruits, dairy, frozen foods) and calculates the average spend per category. This view lets you spot the “money thief” fast – for example, impulse snack purchases that add up to the cost of a weekend outing.

## Avoid impulse buys with budget alerts

FinMoovi’s budget‑alert feature warns you when the running total on your list exceeds the limit you set. When the alert fires, pause, rethink the item, and ask yourself: “Do I really need this, or is it just a fleeting craving?” That tiny mental brake often saves the equivalent of one restaurant meal per month.

## Turn payment routine into a savings habit

After logging the receipt, FinMoovi generates a weekly report that compares the current spend with the average of the past three months. If the trend is upward, the app suggests reduction goals, such as “cut 10% from snacks” or “swap an expensive cut of meat for a cheaper option.” These goals come with automatic [reminders](/en/posts/en-forgot-to-pay-a-bill-how-to-fix-it-in-2026), so you don’t forget to apply the changes.

## Take advantage of sales without falling into traps

Many grocery stores advertise discounts that look irresistible but end up inflating the total bill. Use FinMoovi to scan the barcode of a sale item and compare its unit price with the item already on your list. If the unit price isn’t truly lower, the app flags it as a “misleading sale,” helping you stay focused on real cost‑benefit.
