Your [money disappears](/en/posts/en-do-you-also-lose-track-of-where-your-money-went-at-the-end) because the expenses aren’t recorded and the budget stays invisible; without knowing where every cent went, small purchases pile up and the balance vanishes before the next paycheck. This can happen even if you earn a salary that just covers rent and you keep basic costs in check.

## What’s happening to your money?  
Picture this: you get paid, make a quick plan – “I can afford a coffee a day, rent and transport” – and by the middle of the month your statement shows almost nothing. The feeling that the money “slipped away” is common. That under‑estimation creates a hole in your [cash flow](/en/glossario/en-cash-flow), leaving the balance almost zero before the next payment.

## Why jotting things down on your phone often fails  
Many people try to log expenses in a notes app or a simple [spreadsheet](/en/posts/en-migrating-from-spreadsheets-to-a-financial-app). The problem? The habit breaks quickly. You might forget to open the app, type the wrong amount, or simply lose motivation after a few days. When the routine stops, the “view” of your finances disappears, and so does the ability to adjust your budget.

Ana, our example user, went through this. She used to write every purchase on her phone, but after a few days she stopped recording the tiny grocery items, the quick snack, or that food‑delivery app purchase. When the statement finally appeared, she couldn’t recognize where the “salary money” had gone. The frustration was real, and the solution needed to be as simple as saying “I spent $2.40”.
