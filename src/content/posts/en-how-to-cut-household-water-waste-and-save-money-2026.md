## The real cost of an invisible leak
A small leak may seem insignificant, but over a year it adds up to the water consumption of a small apartment. When water isn’t tracked, the expense becomes a hidden cost that weighs on the [household budget](/en/posts/en-weekly-budget-a-practical-guide-for-those-paid-every-week). Besides the bill, there’s an environmental impact: less water in reservoirs means greater pressure on already scarce resources in many regions.

## 5 habits that waste water and money
1. **Leaving the tap running while brushing teeth** – Every minute left open wastes roughly the volume of a full glass.  
2. **Shower with a leak** – A constant drip can add up to several liters per hour.  
3. **Running the washing machine on short cycles** – Using a full load saves both water and electricity.  
4. **Faulty toilet flush** – A valve that doesn’t close causes incomplete flushes and repeats the cycle.  
5. **Gardening without rainwater reuse** – Watering with tap water when rain could meet the need.

## Practical tips to reduce consumption
**Tip:** *Install sensor‑activated faucets*. They automatically shut off when they detect that water isn’t being used, eliminating the risk of leaving the tap open unintentionally.  
- **Reuse rinse water**: Use the first rinse from laundry to water your plants.  
- **Check for leaks**: Turn off all taps, watch the meter for 30 minutes; any movement indicates a leak.  
- **Adjust shower pressure**: Lowering the temperature a few degrees keeps comfort while cutting usage.  
- **Use eco cycles**: Washers and dishwashers often have “eco” modes that use less water without sacrificing performance.
