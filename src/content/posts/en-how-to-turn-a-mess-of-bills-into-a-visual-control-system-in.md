The fix isn’t to stash everything in different folders or try to remember every expense in your head. ## Let Go of the Paper Clutter

The first step to organizing personal finances is **getting rid of the physical buildup**. Pick a dedicated spot—a box or an envelope—and place there only the documents that still need action (for example, unpaid bills). The rest, like receipts for purchases you’ve already settled, can be shredded or digitized. This simple split already cuts down the time you spend hunting for a specific piece of paper.

> **Practical tip:** Spend five minutes right now sorting everything on your desk into two piles: “to pay” and “already paid.” Toss what you don’t need.

---

## Create Categories That Make Sense

Broad categories like “food” or “transport” are useful, but they can be too vague. Try breaking “food” into “groceries,” “take‑out,” and “coffee shops.” Likewise, “housing” can become “rent,” “utility bills,” and “maintenance.” When your categories mirror your daily reality, your brain instantly knows where each expense belongs, making the visual view in the app much clearer.

## Set Monthly Spending Goals

Clear goals are the engine of financial discipline. For example, decide that “take‑out coffee” shouldn’t cost more than the price of two coffees per day. In FinMoovi you can set a target for each category and receive alerts when you’re close to the limit. This shifts the mindset from “spend until it’s gone” to “spend within the plan.”
