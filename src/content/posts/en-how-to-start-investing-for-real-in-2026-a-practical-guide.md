## Why Investing Matters  

Investing isn’t only for people with a lot of cash. It’s the most efficient way to grow your purchasing power over time, beat inflation, and turn tiny savings into real opportunities. When you put money into assets that earn more than the price‑rise rate, you preserve (and often increase) its real value. That’s the core of any [investment](/en/glossario/en-investment) strategy: **making your money work for you**.

## Investment Options You Can Start Today  

### 1. Index Funds (ETFs)  

![Types of investments you can start today](/images/posts/como-comecar-a-investir-de-verdade-em-2026-guia-pratico-para-2.webp)  

ETFs track the performance of a basket of stocks or bonds, giving you [diversification](/en/glossario/en-diversification) with a single product. Think of them as a fruit basket—you don’t have to pick each fruit individually, but you get a mix that reduces the risk of one bad fruit spoiling the whole basket.

### 2. Government Bonds  

Even without quoting numbers, it’s worth noting that government bonds are considered the safest investments worldwide because they’re backed by the government. They’re ideal for anyone looking for stability and wanting to start with modest contributions.

### 3. Certificates of Deposit (CDs) from Mid‑Size Banks  

CDs usually offer higher returns than a regular savings account and are protected by a deposit insurance fund up to a set limit. They’re a solid entry point for beginners still learning how to handle risk.

### 4. Shares of Established Companies  

Buying stocks can feel intimidating, but focusing on solid companies with a consistent profit history reduces [volatility](/en/glossario/en-volatility). Start with small amounts and increase gradually as you gain confidence.

### 5. Large‑Cap Cryptocurrencies  

If you have a more aggressive profile, the major cryptocurrencies provide upside potential but also higher volatility. Only use money you’re willing to lose and keep the bulk of your portfolio in more stable assets.

## Common Mistakes Beginners Make  

1. **No emergency cushion** – Investing before you have a safety net can force you to sell at bad times.  
2. **Chasing returns only** – Ignoring risk leads to unexpected losses. Balance risk and reward.  
3. **Skipping diversification** – Putting everything into one asset makes you vulnerable to market swings.

## Frequently Asked Questions  

### What is investment risk?  
Risk is the chance that returns will be lower than expected—or that you could lose part of your capital. Each asset class carries a different risk level, and diversification helps mitigate it.

### Do I need to be an expert to invest in ETFs?  
No. ETFs are designed to simplify diversification. Just pick a fund that follows a broad index and invest regularly.

### How do I know if I’m investing enough?  
A good rule of thumb is to allocate at least 10‑15 % of your monthly income to investments after you’ve built an emergency cushion. Adjust the percentage based on your goals and capacity.
