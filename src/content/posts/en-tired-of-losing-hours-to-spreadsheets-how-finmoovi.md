Many of us have spent a weekend trying to log every purchase, utility bill, and unexpected expense in a spreadsheet that never seems to balance. You open Excel, type a number, fix an error, return to real life, and when it’s time to close the sheet, you realize you forgot that last-minute snack or that the water bill hasn’t been entered. The result? Wasted hours, outdated spreadsheets, and the feeling that your money is slipping through your fingers.

### The burden of manual spreadsheets

The truth is most people still use spreadsheets because they believe it’s the only way to gain “visibility” into their finances. But this method has three glaring problems:

1. **Time consumed** – Each entry can take anywhere from 30 seconds to two minutes. Multiply that by dozens of transactions per month, and you’re looking at dozens of hours “stuck” at a keyboard.
2. **Prone to errors** – A transposed digit, a forgotten category, or a copied value can distort the entire picture, leading to poor decisions.
3. **Disconnected from reality** – While you’re at the grocery store, your spreadsheet is at home. By the time you get back, your memory fails and the record is already late.
