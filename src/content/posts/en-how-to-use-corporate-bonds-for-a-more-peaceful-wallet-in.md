Imagine this: you’ve just paid rent, the electricity bill was higher than expected, and you still need to buy that birthday gift you can’t skip. By the end of the month, your account balance seems to vanish like magic, and the anxiety of not knowing how you’ll make it through the next cycle is already knocking at the door. That feeling of “having no control” is more common than you think, and the solution might be closer than you imagine—without having to become a stock‑market expert.

That’s where corporate bonds come in. Unlike a government bond, a corporate bond is a security issued by a company to raise capital. When you buy a corporate bond, you’re lending money to the company and, in return, you receive [interest](/en/glossario/en-interest) payments—usually more attractive than most standard fixed‑income products. The key point for anyone managing a household budget is predictability: you know exactly how much you’ll receive and when, helping you build a payment plan for your bills without surprises.

## Why corporate bonds can be interesting for those seeking safety  

- **Higher yields than average CDs**: Companies often offer rates above government bonds, especially when credit risk is well‑assessed.  
- **[Diversification](/en/glossario/en-diversification)**: Adding corporate bonds to your portfolio reduces reliance on a single income source, balancing risk and return.  
- **Cash‑flow predictability**: Most bonds have set interest‑payment and principal‑repayment dates, making cash‑flow control easier.

These points help create a “cushion” of income that can cover unexpected expenses, like a sudden water‑bill spike or a car repair.

## How to pick a corporate bond without the headache  

1. **Check the credit rating**: International agencies such as Moody’s or S&P assign grades that reflect the issuer’s financial health. A rating of “BBB” or higher is generally a good starting point.  
2. **Look at the maturity date**: Choose bonds whose timelines match your short‑ or medium‑term goals. If you want to finish the next semester without cash strain, aim for maturities within that horizon.  
3. **Consider the [real interest rate](/en/glossario/en-real-interest-rate)**: Compare the offered rate with expected [inflation](/en/glossario/en-inflation) (you can look it up on sites like [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com)). A rate that outpaces inflation preserves future purchasing power.

## Risks and how to mitigate them  

No investment is risk‑free. Corporate bonds carry the credit risk of the issuing company—if the firm runs into trouble, payments could be delayed or stopped. To minimize this risk:

![Risks and how to mitigate them](/images/posts/como-usar-debentures-para-dar-mais-tranquilidade-ao-seu-4.webp)

- **Diversify**: Don’t put all your money into bonds from a single issuer. Spread across different sectors (energy, infrastructure, technology, etc.).  
- **Stay informed**: Use FinMoovi’s news feed, which pushes alerts about significant developments in the companies you’ve invested in.  
- **Keep an emergency fund**: Maintain a separate cash buffer for unexpected events so you don’t have to sell a bond at an unfavorable time.

## Frequently Asked Questions  

### What happens if the issuing company goes bankrupt?  
If the company files for bankruptcy, investors may lose part or all of their capital. That’s why diversification and choosing issuers with solid credit ratings are essential.  

### Are corporate bonds taxed?  
Yes, the earnings are taxed according to the progressive income‑tax schedule for fixed‑income investments. FinMoovi automatically calculates the tax due at the time of redemption.

### How do I know if the offered rate beats inflation?  
Compare the bond’s nominal rate with the expected inflation rate (available from sources like the World Bank). If the real rate (nominal minus inflation) is positive, the investment preserves your purchasing power.
