## Why the shower time matters

Most utilities offer differentiated rates, known as “peak” and “off‑peak” periods. During peak hours—usually late afternoon to early evening—demand spikes and the price per kilowatt‑hour rises. Taking a shower at that time forces your water heater or boiler to work against the grid’s highest load, making the energy more expensive.

Conversely, during low‑demand periods—early morning or late night—the electricity is typically cheaper. Shifting your shower to those windows can noticeably lower [costs](/en/posts/en-private-pension-2026-is-it-worth-it-practical-guide) over months, without changing water temperature or comfort.

## How electricity rates change throughout the day

The exact schedule varies by region, but the principle is universal: the grid experiences usage peaks and calmer stretches. You don’t need to be an expert; just notice when your bill shows a higher amount and match it to the time of use.

![How electricity rates vary during the day](/images/posts/horario-ideal-para-banho-e-economia-de-energia-em-2026-2.webp)

> **Practical tip:** For a week, jot down the times you usually shower and compare them with the total bill amount. If most of the expense falls during peak periods, try moving the shower to before 8 a.m. or after 10 p.m.

## Simple strategies for more economical showers

1. **Cut the time** – Every minute less under the water reduces energy use. A phone timer can be a silent ally.  
2. **Install a low‑flow showerhead** – Devices that save water also cut the energy needed to heat it.  
3. **Use residual heat** – If your water heater is still warm after cooking, route that hot water to the shower before turning the heater back on.  
4. **Adjust the temperature** – Dropping the setting by just one degree makes a difference without sacrificing comfort.

These tiny tweaks, combined with a smarter timing, create a cascade of [savings](/en/glossario/en-savings) that shows up in your [monthly budget](/en/posts/en-how-to-organize-your-monthly-expenses-quickly-and-securely).

### What’s the difference between peak and off‑peak hours?
Peak hours are when the electric grid experiences its highest demand, typically late afternoon to early evening, and rates are higher. Off‑peak hours are periods of lower demand with cheaper tariffs.
