The **SAC Table** (Constant [Amortization](/en/glossario/en-amortization) System) is a method where the portion that reduces the loan principal stays the same each month, while the [interest](/en/glossario/en-interest) portion shrinks as the balance declines. In practice, this means the first [installments](/en/glossario/en-installments) are larger, but they gradually get smaller, easing the pressure on your budget. ## What the SAC Table Is and How It Works
The SAC Table was created to make loan repayment schedules easy to visualize. Unlike the **Price system**, where installments stay the same, SAC keeps the amortization—the part that actually reduces the [debt](/en/glossario/en-debt)—constant. Every month you pay the same amortization amount plus interest calculated on the remaining balance. Because interest is charged on a decreasing balance, the total installment also drops.

**Simple example:**  
- You took a loan roughly equal to one month’s rent.  
- The fixed amortization could be, for instance, 1/12 of the total loan (about 8% of the loan) each month.  
- In the early months, interest (think of it like the cost of a daily coffee) is higher, so the total installment is larger.  
- As the balance shrinks, interest falls, and the final installment can be much lower than the first one.

## How to Calculate the SAC Table in Practice
1. **Identify the total loan amount** (it could be a few months’ salary).  
2. **Set the term** – how many months you plan to pay.  
3. **Calculate the monthly amortization**: divide the total amount by the number of months.  
4. **Calculate each month’s interest**: apply the interest rate to the remaining balance.  
5. **Add amortization + interest** to get that month’s installment.

## Benefits of Choosing the SAC Table
- **Progressive reduction of installments**: great if you expect your income to grow over time or want lighter payments toward the end of the loan.  
- **Transparency**: you see exactly how much interest you’re paying each period, which helps you negotiate better rates.  
- **Easy budgeting**: knowing the installment will drop makes it simpler to fit other expenses, like the electricity bill or grocery shopping, into your plan.

## When the SAC Table Might Not Be the Best Choice
Even though the SAC Table works well in many scenarios, there are cases where the **Price system** (fixed installments) could be a better fit:

- If you prefer **total predictability** with no monthly variations.  
- When the interest rate is very low, the difference between the two systems may be negligible.  
- If [cash flow](/en/glossario/en-cash-flow) is extremely tight in the first months, the higher initial SAC installments might be hard to manage.

## Risks and Precautions When Using the SAC Table
- **Hidden fees**: check for insurance or administrative charges that aren’t included in the nominal interest rate.  
- **Interest adjustments**: some contracts allow the rate to be revised over time; watch for variation clauses.  
- **Initial payment capacity**: make sure the first installment fits your cash flow; otherwise renegotiate the term or consider a different amortization method.

## Frequently Asked Questions
**What does “constant amortization” mean?**  
It’s the fixed part of each installment that reduces the [outstanding balance](/en/glossario/en-outstanding-balance), staying the same throughout the contract.

**How does the SAC Table compare to the Price system?**  
With SAC, installments shrink over time; with Price, they stay equal, which can be easier at the start but usually results in higher total interest paid.

**Can I switch from SAC to Price after the contract starts?**  
It depends on the contract clauses; some lenders allow a switch, but there may be renegotiation costs.
