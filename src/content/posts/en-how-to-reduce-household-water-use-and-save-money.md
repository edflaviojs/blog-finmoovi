## Spotting Hidden Leaks  

The first step to [saving](/en/posts/en-how-to-build-a-budget-that-actually-works-in-2026) water is finding out where the waste is happening. Leaks in faucets, showers, and toilets are the biggest culprits. They can be so subtle they go unnoticed for weeks.

- **Quick test:** Turn off all the taps in the house and watch the water meter. If it keeps moving, there’s a leak somewhere.  
- **Check the toilet:** Drop a few drops of food coloring into the tank. If the water in the bowl changes color without flushing, you have a leak.  
- **Listen:** In a quiet room, hear for the sound of water running. Often the drip’s noise points you right to the source.  

**Practical tip:** **Install aerated faucets** – they mix air with the water, keeping pressure while reducing the volume needed to rinse hands or dishes.

## Tweaking Daily Habits  

Even without leaks, our everyday habits can be major sources of consumption. Small changes can save an amount equivalent to a modest rent payment.

![Adjusting daily habits](/images/posts/como-reduzir-o-consumo-de-agua-em-casa-e-economizar-dinheiro-2.webp)

- **Quick showers:** Cut shower time by two minutes. Use a timer or a music app that signals when the time’s up.  
- **Full loads only:** Run the washing machine at full capacity. Every extra load is water you could have saved.  
- **Reuse rainwater:** Set up a simple barrel to collect rain for watering plants or cleaning outdoors.  

Repeated over a month, these actions add up to [savings](/en/glossario/en-savings) you can redirect to an [emergency fund](/en/glossario/en-emergency-fund) or an investment that brings you peace of mind.

## Technology on Your Side  

Home tech has come a long way and can be a great ally in the mission to cut water use. Here are a few tools worth considering:

- **Flow sensors:** Devices that send alerts to your phone when they detect abnormal consumption.  
- **Smart valves:** They automatically shut off water supply if a leak is detected.  
- **Monitoring apps:** Besides FinMoovi, other platforms let you track consumption history and compare it with global averages. OECD data on residential water use shows that families who monitor their habits can reduce consumption by up to 20 %.
