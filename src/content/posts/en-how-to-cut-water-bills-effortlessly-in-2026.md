## Why the water bill weighs on the budget  

Water is an essential service, yet its price often feels comparable to a daily coffee per person. When consumption spikes—because of leaks, long showers, or wasteful kitchen habits—the bill can swallow a sizable slice of a modest income. According to the Organisation for Economic Co‑operation and Development (OECD), families that adopt simple water‑[saving](/en/posts/en-how-to-build-a-budget-that-actually-works-in-2026) habits can trim their bill by up to **30 %**. That reduction not only eases the wallet but also frees money for other financial goals, like an [emergency fund](/en/glossario/en-emergency-fund) or a long‑term [investment](/en/glossario/en-investment).

## Spotting invisible leaks  

Leaks are the “silent thieves” of water. A faucet that drips one drop per second can waste the equivalent of a whole family’s daily water use in just a month. To locate the problem:

1. **Check the meter** – Record the reading, turn off all water for two hours, then read it again. Any change means there’s a leak.  
2. **Listen** – On a quiet night, the sound of running water can reveal a cracked pipe.

## Simple day‑to‑day tweaks  

### Shorter showers  
Cut shower time by 2 minutes. Each minute saves roughly 10 liters, which adds up to the amount of water in a large soda bottle over a month.

### Mindful dishwashing  
Fill the sink only with the water you need, or run the dishwasher on an eco‑cycle. One full cycle often uses less water than two hand‑wash loads.

### Reusing water  
Collect the water from rinsing vegetables and use it to water plants. This not only saves water but also reduces electricity use by avoiding pump operation.

## Cautions and potential pitfalls  

While saving water is beneficial, some measures can have unintended side effects if not planned properly:

- **Replacing faucets** – Low‑flow devices may lower water pressure, which can be uncomfortable for people with limited mobility.  
- **Reusing water** – Ensure the reused water isn’t contaminated to avoid health issues.  
- **Shutting the main valve** – In emergencies, closing the main valve can stop leaks, but it also leaves the house without water in critical situations (fires, basic needs).

Assess each action, and if you’re unsure, consult a professional before making structural changes.
