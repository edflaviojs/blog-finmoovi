The most energy‑efficient temperature for an air conditioner is between **23 °C and 26 °C** (73‑79 °F). In this range the unit runs smoothly and can reduce electricity use by up to **30 %** without sacrificing comfort.

Imagine this: after a long day you walk through the front door and feel the hot hallway air. Instinctively you crank the air conditioner down to **18 °C** hoping to cool the room fast. Within minutes the compressor kicks in, the noise spikes, and when the monthly electricity bill arrives it looks like it was written in invisible ink – but the numbers are very real. That “I spent too much” feeling is more common than you think, and most people don’t realize how much the thermostat setting influences consumption.

### Why the temperature matters  
Cooling systems work against the temperature difference between inside and outside. The larger the gap, the harder the compressor has to work, and the more electricity it draws. When the thermostat is set too low, the unit cycles on and off rapidly, raising consumption by roughly **10 %‑25 %** for each degree below the recommended range.

### Recommended temperature range  
For most warm and subtropical climates, **23 °C‑26 °C** is the “golden zone.” In this band:

- The compressor runs in a stable mode, avoiding frequent starts and stops.  
- Electrical demand drops because the motor’s workload is lighter.  
- Thermal comfort is still maintained, especially if you have cross‑ventilation or curtains that block direct sun.

If the outdoor temperature climbs above **30 °C** (86 °F), you might be tempted to lower the thermostat even more. Instead, use a ceiling or floor fan to circulate air; this lets you keep the thermostat around **24 °C** while still feeling a pleasant breeze.

### How each degree impacts your bill  
Think of your electricity bill as comparable to a typical rent payment. Cutting consumption by **20 %** is like [saving](/en/posts/en-how-to-build-a-budget-that-actually-works-in-2026) a quarter of that rent. Practically, every degree you lower the thermostat below the sweet spot adds an extra cost similar to a daily coffee for an entire month.

Extra consumption also speeds up wear and tear, shortening the unit’s lifespan by up to **3 years**—a replacement cost comparable to a high‑end smartphone.

### Tips to optimise your air conditioner  

1. **Keep doors and windows closed** while the unit is running; leaks force the system to work harder.  
2. **Use curtains or blinds** to block direct sunlight, especially during peak hours.  
3. **Clean filters regularly** – a dirty filter can reduce efficiency by up to **15 %**.  
4. **Activate “eco” or “sleep” mode** at night; the system automatically adjusts to a more economical temperature range.  
5. **Combine fans with the air conditioner**; moving air lets you set the thermostat around **24 °C** without feeling hot.  
6. **Smart scheduling**: program automatic shut‑off when nobody is home.

### Risks and cautions  

- **Thermal discomfort**: setting the temperature too low can cause discomfort, colds, or headaches. Prioritise your well‑being before adjusting the thermostat.  
- **Neglected maintenance**: skipping filter cleaning or periodic service can erase any savings.  
- **Over‑reliance on cooling**: depending solely on the air conditioner during prolonged heat waves can drive costs up; always combine with natural ventilation when possible.
