Have you opened a bank account, made the deposit you promised yourself, and then realized you have no idea how much you’ve actually saved for that trip, a car down‑payment, or an [emergency fund](/en/glossario/en-emergency-fund)? That feeling of “I put money in, but I can’t see anything” is more common than you think. Most people set [savings](/en/glossario/en-savings) goals, but the progress gets lost among spreadsheets, paper notes, or just memory. The result? Discouragement, postponed dreams, and often the decision to abandon the goal because it seems the effort isn’t worth it.

## The weight of uncertainty: when a goal becomes a mystery  

Imagine Alex, who wants to save roughly twice the monthly rent to make a car down‑payment. He creates a “envelope” in his pocket, tucks away the leftover salary, and occasionally deposits the cash into his account. Between an [electricity bill](/en/posts/en-how-to-cut-your-electricity-bill-and-gain-peace-of-mind) and a birthday gift, the account balance goes up and down, and Alex can’t see how close he really is to the target. He checks the statement, counts the deposits in a spreadsheet, and still feels like he’s “flying blind.” This lack of clarity creates two main pains:

1. **Anxiety** – Every time the statement shows a lower‑than‑expected number, his heart skips a beat.  
2. **Misaligned pace** – Without knowing if he’s moving fast enough, Alex might overspend or, conversely, become so rigid that his quality of life suffers.

According to the *World Bank*, families that don’t have a clear visual way to follow their financial goals postpone medium‑term investments by up to 30 %. In other words, the missing visual cue isn’t just annoying—it’s a real blocker to progress.
