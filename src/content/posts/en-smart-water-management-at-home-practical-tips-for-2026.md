Imagine this: after a hectic day you get home, turn on the tap to wash your hands, and the water keeps running. The constant drip echoes in the kitchen, and when you check the water bill you see a charge that’s almost double the rent of a modest apartment. Seeing money flow out with the water is an instant gut punch, and the frustration is real. It happens more often than we’d like, but the good news is that a few easy steps—and the right tool—can turn that leak of resources into a steady stream of [savings](/en/glossario/en-savings).

### Identify where water is slipping from your budget
The first move is to spot the critical consumption points. Too often, excess use hides in habits we barely notice: dripping faucets, leaky toilets, showers that run for ages, and even washing more clothes than needed. Once you know who the “water thieves” are, taking action becomes much simpler.

Your water bill can also reveal usage patterns you didn’t expect. A sudden spike may point to a hidden pipe leak. By digging into the consumption history you can see monthly trends and compare periods, making it easier to decide where to cut back.

### 5 habits that cut water use without sacrifice
1. **Turn the tap off while brushing your teeth** – the water you save is less than the cost of a modest dinner out.  
2. **Install aerators on showerheads** – they reduce flow while keeping the feeling of pressure.  
3. **Run full laundry cycles** – wait for the machine to fill before starting; this cuts the number of washes each month.  
4. **Reuse rainwater** for watering plants or cleaning the yard; a bucket of rainwater can replace several bottled purchases.  
5. **Check for leaks regularly** – a tiny “drip‑drip” can add up to the cost of a small rent over a year.

## FAQ
**How can I spot leaks without hiring a plumber?**  
Watch for a rising water bill without any change in your habits. A simple test: close all taps, note the meter reading, and leave it for a few hours. If the number moves, you have a leak.

**What’s the best way to reduce water use in the shower?**  
Install a showerhead aerator and shave a few minutes off your shower time. You’ll see a noticeable drop in usage without sacrificing cleanliness.
