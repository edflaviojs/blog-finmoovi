### What is the PRICE formula?  
The PRICE formula, also known as the French [amortization](/en/glossario/en-amortization) system, calculates the payment amount (**P**) of a loan or [financing](/en/glossario/en-financing) with [compound interest](/en/glossario/en-compound-interest):

\[
P = \frac{V \times i}{1 - (1 + i)^{-n}}
\]

- **V** – [present value](/en/glossario/en-present-value) (the amount you’re financing)  
- **i** – interest rate per period (monthly, for example)  
- **n** – total number of installments  

This equation guarantees that every installment has the same value, making [monthly budgeting](/en/posts/en-discover-a-simple-app-to-organize-your-finances-in-2026) a breeze.

### How to apply the formula in practice  
Suppose you want to finance a home appliance that costs about three minimum wages (roughly **$1,200**). The store offers 12 installments at **2 % per month**. Here’s how to use the formula:

1. **Identify V** – the total purchase price (≈ $1,200).  
2. **Calculate i** – 2 % per month = 0.02.  
3. **Set n** – 12 installments.  
4. **Plug the numbers into the formula** to get the monthly payment.

### Why PRICE can be better than “interest‑free” offers  
Many merchants advertise “interest‑free,” but they often embed hidden costs (like a higher upfront price) that effectively act as interest. Using the PRICE formula lets you compare:

- **Interest‑free**: simple division of the total amount (V / n).  
- **With interest**: the real calculation using the effective rate.

If the two payments are close, the “interest‑free” deal may be fine. If not, it’s worth renegotiating or looking for another payment method.

### Risks and cautions when using the PRICE formula  
- **High interest rate**: even a low‑looking payment can hide a total cost far above the cash price.  
- **Income commitment**: if installments consume more than half of your disposable income, you risk default.  
- **Rate changes**: some contracts have variable rates. Make sure the rate is fixed before you sign.  
- **[Multi‑currency](/en/posts/en-digital-bank-vs-major-bank-which-is-worth-it-in-2026) purchases**: if you buy abroad, consider exchange‑rate fluctuations. FinMoovi lets you simulate installments in different currencies and see the effect of the current rate.

## Frequently Asked Questions  

**How does the PRICE formula differ from the “Price Table” used by banks?**  
The “Price Table” is simply the application of the PRICE formula to bank credit. The name refers to the tabular presentation, but the [underlying](/en/glossario/en-underlying) math is identical.  

**Can I use FinMoovi’s calculator for installments in other currencies?**

**What if the store doesn’t disclose the interest rate?**  
Use a market‑average rate for the type of product (e.g., 1.5 %–2 % per month for appliances). FinMoovi provides a reference based on OECD and World Bank data.  

**Does the calculator consider compound interest?**  
Absolutely. The PRICE formula incorporates compound interest, which is the standard practice for most financing and installment plans.
