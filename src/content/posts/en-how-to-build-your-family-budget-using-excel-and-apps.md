## Why Excel Still Makes Sense

Many think spreadsheets are a thing of the past, replaced by “ready‑made” apps. Yet Excel remains the most flexible tool for anyone who wants to customize categories, create dynamic charts, and, above all, keep total control of the data without relying on a constant internet connection.

- **Full visibility**: you can see all income and expenses in a single view, drag rows, and build pivot tables as needed.  
- **Adaptability**: if the family grows or a new expense appears, just add a column or row – no waiting for software updates.  
- **Privacy**: the data stays on your computer or in the cloud you choose, with no third‑party access to your finances.

These points make Excel the solid “heart,” while FinMoovi acts as the “brain” feeding the spreadsheet with real‑time information.

## Step‑by‑Step Guide to Building Your Family Budget

1. **List every source of income** – salary, side‑hustle earnings, pension. Create a column called “Income.”  
2. **Identify fixed expenses** – rent, water bills, internet. Group them under categories like “Housing,” “Utilities.”  
3. **Separate variable costs** – groceries, entertainment, transport. Give each item a sub‑category for easier later analysis.  
4. **Set monthly goals** – how much you want to save, how much you can spend in each category. Add “Goal” rows right below each group.  
5. **Build a dashboard** – use pie or bar charts to show the proportion each category represents of total spending.

![Step‑by‑step guide to building your family budget](/images/posts/como-montar-seu-orcamento-familiar-usando-excel-e-apps-2.webp)

By the end of the first week you’ll have a clear view of where the money is being consumed. If something looks off, adjust the goal or the category. The key is to keep the habit of updating the spreadsheet daily.

## **Practical Tip:** Use Colors to Flag Goals

After entering income and expenses, pick a color for each goal (e.g., green for “under budget,” yellow for “watch,” red for “over”). In Excel, apply conditional formatting: if a category total exceeds its goal, the cell turns red. This quick visual cue helps you spot, at a glance, where the budget is at risk.

![Practical tip: use colors to flag goals](/images/posts/como-montar-seu-orcamento-familiar-usando-excel-e-apps-4.webp)

Beyond aesthetics, coloring creates immediate feedback that reinforces financial discipline. When the color changes, you instantly know it’s time to review that expense.
