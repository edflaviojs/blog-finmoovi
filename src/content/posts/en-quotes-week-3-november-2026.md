## Weekly Summary of the Brazilian Financial Market (Nov 9 to 16, 2026)

The week went by quickly, but it brought a few points that every retail investor needs to understand. I’ll split the overview into four blocks: how the dollar and euro behaved, what the [Selic](/en/glossario/en-selic) is saying about your portfolio, a practical tip to apply today, and finally what to watch for next week. All in a simple way, without complicated terms and with everyday examples.

### 1. Dollar and Euro – Week’s Trend

- **Dollar:** The exchange rate stayed at **R$ 5.66** for most of the week. No variations were announced in the bulletins we receive, so we can consider that the price of the [currency](/en/glossario/en-moeda) stayed stable for those who follow the quote in the bank’s app or on news sites.
- **Euro:** The euro was quoted at **R$ 6.25**. Like the dollar, there were no reports of sharp swings, indicating a relatively calm scenario for those with expenses or investments tied to the European currency.

For anyone with accounts in dollars or euros, stability is good news: there’s no surprise when converting to reais. If you usually buy imported products or plan to travel, you can use the predictability to schedule your spending without fearing exchange-rate shocks.

### 2. Selic and Impact on Investments

The basic interest rate – the Selic – remains at **13.75% per year** (according to the latest Copom decision on 09/12/2026). This high rate is still the main factor that attracts investors to fixed income, such as CDBs, [Tesouro Direto](/en/glossario/en-tesouro-direto) and LCIs/LCAs. When the Selic is at this level, securities that pay close to this index usually offer more attractive returns than savings accounts and even some fixed‑income funds.

**What does this mean in practice?**  
- If you have money saved in a fixed‑income fund that yields 12% per year, it may be time to compare it with a [CDB](/en/glossario/en-cdb) that pays 13% or more, since the difference can be significant over a few years.  
- For those who prefer the safety of a savings account, it’s worth looking at fixed‑income alternatives that track the Selic, because savings pays less when the rate is high.
