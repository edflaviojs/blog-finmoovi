### What compound interest looks like in everyday life  
Compound interest is simply earning money on the money you’ve already earned. Think of a plant that grows a bit each month; the next month you water not only the original plant but also the new growth, and it keeps getting bigger faster.

If you invest **about $200** (roughly a minimum‑wage salary in many countries) in an account that compounds interest, after a few months the amount will be higher than if you just added the same $200 every month without any return. The difference appears because each period’s earnings are calculated on the total balance, not just the initial contribution.

### Quick way to calculate the return without a spreadsheet  
1. **Set the starting amount** – the cash you have saved right now.  
2. **Pick an annual return rate** – use a low‑risk [investment](/en/glossario/en-investment) rate (for example, a [fixed‑income fund](/en/posts/en-fixed-income-funds-a-simple-path-to-financial-security) that yields around **5 % per year**, according to [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com)). Convert it to the period you’re analyzing (monthly, daily, etc.).  
3. **Choose the number of periods** – how many months or years you plan to keep the money invested.  
4. **Apply the formula**:

   \[
   \text{Future Value} = \text{Initial Amount} \times (1 + \text{rate})^{\text{number of periods}}
   \]
