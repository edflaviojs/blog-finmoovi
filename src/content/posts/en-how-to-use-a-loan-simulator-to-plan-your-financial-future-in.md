Imagine the scene: you’ve just received the paperwork for a new loan and, when you open the statement, you see two columns of numbers that seem to change every month. Your heart races, anxiety kicks in. You think, “Will I be able to pay everything without squeezing my budget too hard?” That feeling of uncertainty is common when stepping into the world of financing, especially if you’re not yet clear on the difference between the SAC and PRICE [amortization](/en/glossario/en-amortization) methods.

**What is a SAC/PRICE simulator?**  
Instead of a technical dictionary, let’s explain it with everyday language. Think of the simulator as that friend who, before you buy a car or remodel the house, does the math on how each installment will hit your wallet.  

- In the **SAC (Constant Amortization System)**, the part that actually reduces the debt—the amortization—is fixed, while the interest portion shrinks over time. The result: the first [installments](/en/glossario/en-installments) are higher, then they gradually decline.  
- In the **PRICE (French amortization)**, the installments are always the same; what changes is the mix of interest and amortization within each payment. At the start, most of the payment is interest; only later does the amortization share grow as the [outstanding balance](/en/glossario/en-outstanding-balance) falls.

### Why simulate before you sign?
- **Realistic view**: You see exactly how much each month will eat into your budget, no guesswork.  
- **Easy comparison**: Switching from SAC to PRICE (or vice‑versa) with a click shows the cash‑flow impact.  
- **Surprise prevention**: Avoid the classic “I didn’t realize the installment would increase” that often leads to unexpected debt.

### When SAC is usually the better choice
- **Fast payoff goal**: If you want to shrink the debt quickly, SAC’s decreasing installments lighten the load in later years.  
- **[Variable income](/en/glossario/en-variable-income)**: Freelancers, commission‑based sellers, or anyone whose earnings grow over time can handle higher early payments and benefit from the gradual decline.

### When PRICE can be the smart pick
- **Fixed budget**: If your monthly [cash flow](/en/glossario/en-cash-flow) has little wiggle room (e.g., you’re paying rent that’s roughly a minimum‑wage equivalent), equal installments help you plan without shocks.  
- **Long‑term [planning](/en/posts/en-budgeting-with-minimum-wage-practical-tips)**: When the loan funds an [investment](/en/glossario/en-investment) that will generate steady returns (like buying work equipment), the predictability of equal payments makes cash‑flow projection easier.

### Avoiding common pitfalls
- **Don’t focus only on the total amount**: A loan may look cheap because it has lower overall interest, but if the early installments are as high as a monthly rent, the risk of missed payments rises.  
- **Watch out for extra costs**: Opening fees, insurance, and other charges can change the picture. FinMoovi lets you input these values for a complete calculation.  
- **Revisit the plan**: Life changes. Every six months, capture the proposal again and tweak the variables; the app will tell you whether switching from SAC to PRICE (or the other way around) still makes sense.
