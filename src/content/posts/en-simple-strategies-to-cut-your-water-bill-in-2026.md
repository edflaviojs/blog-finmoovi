Imagine this: it’s the end of the month, you open the water bill and the amount looks like a rent payment. The nightly drip of a leaky faucet feels like a constant reminder that every drop is draining your budget. Good news—simple changes and a little tech support can turn that around.

## Why it’s worth cutting water use  

Water isn’t just a natural resource; it’s a direct line item on your monthly expenses and an environmental factor that touches us all. According to the **OECD**, per‑capita domestic water use is trending upward in urban areas, squeezing both wallets and ecosystems. Cutting waste brings three immediate benefits:

1. **More money in the budget** – fewer liters mean a smaller bill.  
2. **Peace of mind** – fewer surprises when the statement arrives.  
3. **Environmental help** – less stress on reservoirs and treatment plants.  

## Practical tips you can start today  

Here are seven actions you can implement in a few minutes, with little to no investment.

### 1. Reuse washing‑machine water  

When the cycle ends, the water is still clean. Use it to mop floors, water plants, or fill the toilet tank. One bucket can save dozens of liters over a month.

### 2. Install aerators on taps  

Aerators mix air into the flow, cutting water use by up to 50 % without changing the feel of pressure. They cost a few cents and can be swapped in under a minute. Result: less water while brushing teeth or washing hands.

### 3. Put an end to long showers  

A five‑minute shower uses far less water than a fifteen‑minute one. Try turning the tap off while you soap up and only reopening to rinse. Need a timer? Set an alarm on your phone—5 minutes is enough.

### 4. Run the washing machine only with full loads  

Waiting for a full load boosts water‑use efficiency. If you have only a few items, choose the “quick” or “eco” cycle, which uses less water and energy.

### 5. Check for leaks with a simple test  

Close all home valves and watch the meter for 30 minutes. If the reading moves, you have a hidden leak. Fix dripping taps or toilets that don’t close fully right away. Small repairs prevent thousands of liters of loss each year.

### 6. Try “dry wash” for fruits and vegetables  

When cleaning produce, soak them in a bowl, then give a quick rinse. For fruits, use a colander and rinse in a cup, reusing the water for plants. This cuts down on running‑water waste.
