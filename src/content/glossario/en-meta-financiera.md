## What is a Financial Goal?

Financial goal is a concrete objective for your money. It's not "I want to save more" — it's "I want to gather R$ 10,000 by December 2025 to make a down payment on a car". The more specific, the higher the probability of achieving it.

## How to define good goals (SMART method)

- **S**pecific: "Gather R$ 10,000" (not "save more")
- **M**easurable: [valor](/es/glossario/es-valor-financiero) exact value you can track
- **A**chievable: possible with your current income
- **R**elevant: important to you
- **T**ime-bound: with a defined deadline

## Goal examples

### Short term (up to 1 year)

- Build an emergency reserve of R$ 15,000
- Pay off a [crédito](/es/posts/es-tarjeta-de-credito-vs-tarjeta-de-debito-cual-vale-mas-la-pen) card of R$ 3,000
- Gather R$ 5,000 for a trip

### Medium term (1-5 years)

- Gather R$ 50,000 for a down payment on an apartment
- Change car (R$ 30,000)
- Do a trade-in (R$ 20,000)

### Long term (5+ years)

- [Independencia financiera](/es/glossario/es-independencia-financiera) (R$ 1.500.000)
- [Jubilación](/es/posts/es-prevision-privada-2026-vale-la-pena-guia-practica) complementary
- Buy a property outright

## How to accompany

1. Define the total amount and the timeframe
2. Divide by the number of months (required monthly contribution)
3. Automate the monthly transfer
4. Track progress (apps like [FinMoovi](/es/posts/es-5-alternativas-al-mobills-en-2026) help)
5. Celebrate intermediate milestones (25 %, 50 %, 75 %)

## Advice

Keep a maximum of 3 goals simultaneously. Too many goals split your focus and money — none progress fast enough to keep you motivated.
