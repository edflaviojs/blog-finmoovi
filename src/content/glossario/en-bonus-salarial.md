## What is

A bonus is an extra payment that the company offers to the employee, in addition to the fixed salary. It can appear as a reward for meeting targets, profit sharing, or even as an incentive to stay with the company. Unlike the salary, which is guaranteed every month, the bonus depends on factors such as individual performance, team results, or the company's own results. In simple terms, think of the bonus as that “extra money” you receive when you make an extra effort or when the company has a good result.

![O que é](/images/glossario/bonus-inline-1.webp)



## How it works

When the company decides to pay bonuses, it usually defines clear rules so everyone knows what needs to be done. These rules can vary a lot, but they generally follow some patterns:

- **Performance target:** You meet a sales or production target and receive a percentage of the salary as a bonus.  
- **Profit sharing (PLR):** If the company made a profit in the quarter, part of that profit is distributed among the employees.  
- **Retention bonus:** A [valor](/glossario/valor-financeiro) paid to those who agree to stay with the company for a set period.  
- **Year‑end bonus:** An extra payment at the end of the year, often tied to the company's overall performance.

## Advantages

Receiving bonuses brings [benefícios](/posts/cdb-vs-tesouro-selic-qual-rende-mais-em-2026) both for the worker and the company. Among the main advantages are:

![Vantagens](/images/glossario/bonus-inline-3.webp)

- **Extra motivation:** The extra money encourages seeking better results.  
- **Recognition:** You feel your effort is being valued.  
- **Income increase:** A bonus of 10% on a salary of R$5,000 can represent R$500 more in the pocket.  
- **Financial flexibility:** The amount can be used to pay off debts, invest, or take that trip that was on the list.

## Risks

Despite the advantages, it’s important to stay aware of possible risks associated with bonuses:

- **Instability:** Since the bonus is not guaranteed, relying on it for fixed expenses can be risky.  
- **Pressure to meet targets:** Very aggressive goals can generate stress and even compromise health.  
- **Inequality:** If the bonus policy is not transparent, it can cause dissatisfaction among colleagues.  
- **Taxation:** The bonus enters the calculation base of [Imposto de Renda](/glossario/imposto-de-renda), so part of it will be deducted at the time.

## Practical examples

Let’s see how the bonus can impact people earning between R$3,000 and R$8,000 per month:

- **John, salary R$3,500:** Receives a 5% bonus for meeting the sales target. 5% of R$3,500 = R$175. At the end of the month, he has R$3,675 to spend.  
- **Maria, salary R$6,000:** The company distributes 10% of profit as PLR. She receives R$600 bonus, which she decides to apply to an [fundo de emergência](/ferramentas/calculadora-reserva).  
- **Peter, salary R$8,000:** Gets a retention bonus of R$1,200 for signing a 2‑year stay contract. He uses part to pay the down payment of a car and the rest invests in [ações](/glossario/acoes).  

**Practical tip:** If your bonus is variable, set aside at least 50% of it in a reserve account before thinking about spending.  
**Practical tip:** Use the bonus to pay off high‑interest [juros](/glossario/juros) debts first; this way you save more in the long run.  
**Practical tip:** When the bonus is taxed, calculate the net amount (after tax) so you’re not surprised when planning your budget.

## How to start

If you still don’t receive bonuses or want to improve how you handle them, follow these steps:

- **Understand your company's policy:** Ask HR or your manager how the calculation works and what the targets are.  
- **Set personal goals:** Align your objectives with the company's targets to increase chances of receiving the bonus.  
- **Create a usage plan:** Decide beforehand whether you’ll invest, save, or spend the extra money.  
- **Track performance:** Use spreadsheets or apps of financial control to monitor your progress and the expected bonus amount.  
- **Negotiate when needed:** If you feel the targets are unfair, talk to your manager and propose adjustments.
