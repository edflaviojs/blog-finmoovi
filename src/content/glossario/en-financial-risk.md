## What is financial risk?
Financial risk is the chance that something unexpected will hit your wallet and make it harder to meet your goals. It can be a sudden drop in income, an unplanned medical bill, a [credit](/en/glossario/en-credit)‑card charge you didn’t notice, or even a currency swing if you travel abroad. In plain language, it’s the “what‑if” that keeps you up at night when you look at your bank balance and wonder whether you’ll have enough for the next rent, the grocery list, or that weekend getaway.

## Why it matters in everyday life
Imagine you’ve just paid the monthly rent, bought a few groceries, and are about to treat yourself to a coffee that costs about the price of a daily cup. Suddenly, your phone buzzes: a credit‑card statement shows a charge you don’t recognize. You scramble to find the receipt, but it’s buried in a pile of paper. By the time you sort it out, the due date is near and you risk a late‑fee that could have been avoided. This scenario illustrates three common sources of financial risk:

![What is financial risk?](/images/glossario/risco-financeiro-inline-1.webp)



- **Unexpected expenses** – medical visits, car repairs, or a broken appliance.
- **Cash‑flow gaps** – periods when your income is lower than usual, such as after a freelance project ends.
- **Currency exposure** – spending abroad or buying foreign‑currency assets that can lose value.

If you don’t see these risks coming, they can quickly turn a stable budget into a stressful scramble.
