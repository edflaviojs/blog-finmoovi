## What it is

A loan is a contract in which an institution (bank, fintech or individual) provides a cash amount to the borrower, who commits to return that amount plus interest (the cost of [crédito](/en/glossario/en-credito)) in pre‑defined installments. In simple terms, it’s like borrowing money from a friend, but with formal rules and a “rent” charge for using that money.

![What it is](/images/glossario/emprestimo-inline-1.webp)



## How it works

When you apply for a loan, the institution analyzes your payment capacity (income, credit history and financial commitments). If approved, the amount is released and you start paying the installments, which include part of the principal (the money you borrowed) and part of the interest (the cost of credit). The process usually follows these steps:

- **Credit analysis:** checks your score and whether the bills fit your budget.  
- **Term definition:** choose between 6, 12, 24 or more months, depending on the amount and your income.  
- **Contract signing:** document that details interest rate, CET (Total Effective Cost) and late payment penalties.  
- **Money release:** the amount lands in your account within up to 24 hours in most fintechs.  
- **Monthly payments:** automatic debit or boleto, always on the agreed date.

## Advantages

A loan can be the solution when you need quick cash and have a clear plan for its use. Among the positives, the following stand out:

![Advantages](/images/glossario/emprestimo-inline-3.webp)



- **Immediate liquidity:** the money reaches the account in a few minutes, ideal for emergencies.  
- **Flexible term:** you choose the number of installments that fit your budget.  
- **Competitive rates:** fintechs usually offer lower interest than credit card revolving credit.  
- **Building credit history:** paying on time helps improve your score, making future financing easier.

## Risks

Despite the advantages, every credit carries risks that can compromise your budget if you’re not careful:

- **Accumulated interest:** the longer the term, the higher the total paid in interest.  
- **Late payment penalties:** delays generate fines and increase the outstanding balance.  
- **Over‑indebtedness:** using the loan to pay other debts can create a debt cycle.  
- **High CET:** the Total Effective Cost includes fees, insurance and IOF (Tax on Financial Operations), which can raise the final cost.

## Practical examples

Imagine you earn R$4.500 per month and need to remodel the kitchen. You decide to take a loan of R$15.000 with an interest rate of 2.5% per month, to be paid in 24 installments. The simplified calculation yields a payment of approximately R$860. See how this impacts your budget:

- Gross salary: R$4.500  
- Fixed expenses (rent, bills, food): R$2.200  
- Loan installment: R$860  
- Remaining for other expenses and savings: R$1.440  

If the same person has a salary of R$7.200 and wants to buy a car for R$30.000, they can opt for 36 installments of R$1.200 (interest rate of 1.8% per month). The remaining budget still allows saving R$1.500 per month for emergencies.

## How to start

To open your first loan safely, follow these practical steps:

- **Check your score:** access your registration on sites like Serasa or Boa Vista; a score above 600 already eases approval.  
- **Calculate the installment:** use the calculator in your bank or fintech app; input amount, term and rate to know the exact monthly payment.  
- **Compare offers:** don’t settle for the first proposal; compare interest, CET and payment conditions at least three institutions.  
- **Create a payment plan:** before signing, write down how many installments fit your budget without compromising the emergency reserve.  
- **Sign digitally:** most platforms allow electronic signature, making the process fast and paperless.

**Practical tip:** before closing, simulate the installment’s impact on your cash flow using a simple spreadsheet or the “budget” feature of your finance app.  
**Practical tip:** if the loan is to pay off debts, choose a term that makes the new installment lower than the sum of the old ones; that way you truly save.  
**Practical tip:** set up automatic debit to avoid forgetfulness and fines; most banks allow you to choose the payment day that coincides with your salary receipt.
