## What is it

The **bonus** is an extra compensation that a company gives to the employee, apart from the base salary. It is not a fixed payment; it usually depends on achieved goals, company results, or special events like year‑end. In simple terms, it’s like an “extra” that they thank you for your effort, and it can appear in the bank account as a one‑time sum or as part of an incentive plan.

![Qué es](/images/glossario/bonus-inline-1.webp)



## How it works

The bonus mechanism varies according to each company’s policy, but it generally follows these steps:

- The company defines clear goals (sales, productivity, cost savings, etc.).  
- Each quarter or year the results are evaluated and compared to those goals.  
- If the employee meets or exceeds the objectives, the [value](/es/glossario/es-valor-financiero) of the bonus is calculated based on a pre‑established percentage of the salary or of the generated profit.  
- The payment is made through payroll or as a separate credit, and it is usually subject to taxes like any other income.

In practice, if you earn a salary of R$5,000 and the company sets a bonus of 10 % for reaching the sales target, you will receive an extra [R$500](/es/posts/es-montando-cartera-diversificada-con-r-500) at the end of the period.

## Advantages

The bonus brings several [benefits](/es/posts/es-cdb-vs-tesouro-selic-cual-rinde-mas-en-2026) both for the company and for the employee:

![Ventajas](/images/glossario/bonus-inline-3.webp)



- **Motivation:** Having an additional financial goal drives you to give your best day to day.  
- **Recognition:** Rewards effort and productivity, creating a more positive work environment.  
- **Financial flexibility:** Allows covering unexpected expenses or saving without affecting the monthly budget.  
- **Talent retention:** Employees who receive bonuses regularly tend to stay longer with the company.

## Risks

Although the bonus sounds like a blessing, it also involves some risks you should know:

- **Dependence:** If you get used to receiving frequent bonuses, you might overestimate your income and overspend.  
- **Variability:** Bonuses are not guaranteed; in bad years the company may reduce or eliminate the payment.  
- **Taxes:** Since it is taxable income, a part of the bonus goes to the tax authority, reducing the net amount.  
- **Pressure:** Goals can generate stress if they are too ambitious or unrealistic.

## Practical examples

Let’s see how the bonus impacts different real salaries:

- **Salary R$3,000:** The company offers a 5 % bonus for meeting quarterly goals. If you achieve it, you receive R$150 extra each quarter, totaling R$600 per year.  
- **Salary R$5,500:** A bonus of 8 % is set when closing the fiscal year with profits. Good performance generates a R$440 bonus, which you can use to pay a debt or invest.  
- **Salary R$8,000:** The company has a profit‑sharing plan of 12 % for teams that exceed their objectives. If your team achieves the target, the bonus would be R$960, enough for a short trip or to boost your [emergency fund](/es/glossario/es-fondo-de-emergencia).

**Practical tip:** **If you receive a bonus, allocate at least 30 % to your savings or investment before thinking about spending it.**  

**Practical tip:** **Always check the payslip and verify that the bonus calculation matches what was agreed; errors are more common than you think.**

## How to start

If your company already offers bonuses but you’re not making the most of them, follow these steps:

- Ask Human Resources or your direct manager what the exact policy is (goals, percentages, payment dates).  
- Write the goals in your agenda and break down the concrete actions you need to take to achieve them.  
- When you receive the bonus, separate the money into three envelopes: immediate spending, savings, and reinvestment.  
- Use a tracking tool (a [spreadsheet](/es/posts/es-migrando-de-la-hoja-de-calculo-a-una-app-financiera) or an app) to record each bonus and its destination.  
- Evaluate at the end of the year whether the bonuses really improved your financial situation and adjust your strategy.
