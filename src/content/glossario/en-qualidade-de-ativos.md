## What is 

Quality, in the world of finance, is not an abstract concept; it is the ability of an investment, credit, or financial product to maintain its value over time. When we talk about "quality", we are evaluating the risk of loss, the solidity of the company or asset, and the predictability of returns. In simple terms, a high-quality asset has a lower chance of depreciating unexpectedly and usually generates more stable income.

![What is  ](/images/glossario/qualidade-inline-1.webp)



## How it works 

Quality is measured by indicators such as risk classification (rating), the financial health of the company, and the consistency of profits. For example, shares of companies with an AAA rating are considered high-quality because they have a history of paying [dividends](/glossario/dividendos) and low [volatility](/glossario/volatilidade). When you buy a debt title (such as a CDB), the quality of the issuer determines the interest rate it pays: the higher the quality, the lower the rate, since the risk of default is low.

- **Rating**: note given by agencies (e.g., Moody's, Fitch) that indicates the probability of default.
- **[Profit margin](/glossario/margem-de-lucro)**: companies with high margins tend to be more resilient.
- **Liquidity**: high-quality assets are easy to sell without losing much value.

## Advantages 

Investing in high-quality assets brings clear benefits to those seeking financial stability.

![Advantages  ](/images/glossario/qualidade-inline-3.webp)



- **Lower volatility**: prices fluctuate less, protecting your assets in times of crisis.
- **Predictable income**: dividends or interest are more consistent, making it easier to plan your budget.
- **Easy access to credit**: those who have high-quality investments can use these assets as collateral, obtaining loans with lower interest rates.

These advantages are especially useful for those with a salary between R$3,000 and R$8,000 who need to balance monthly expenses, [emergency fund](/glossario/reserva-de-emergencia), and long-term goals.

## Risks 

Even high-quality assets are not risk-free. Ignoring these points can compromise your financial plan.

- **Market risk**: macroeconomic changes (inflation, interest rates) can affect the value of "safe" assets.
- **Concentration**: putting all your money in a single type of high-quality asset can limit potential gains.
- **Reduced return rate**: since the risk is lower, the return tends to be lower compared to riskier investments.

Therefore, it is essential to balance quality with [diversification](/glossario/diversificacao) to avoid being "stuck" with modest returns.

## Practical examples 

Let's see how quality translates into the daily life of those who earn between R$3,000 and R$8,000.

- **Case 1 – Salary R$4,500**: João decides to allocate 20% of his income (R$900) to a fixed-income fund with securities from large banks (AAA rating). He guarantees a return of 6% per year, sufficient to strengthen his emergency fund.
- **Case 2 – Salary R$7,200**: Maria invests R$1,200 monthly in shares of companies with an AA rating, which pay dividends of 4% per year. Even with market volatility, she receives around R$48 in dividends per month, helping to pay the electricity bill.
- **Case 3 – Salary R$5,800**: Carlos uses the FinMoovi app to buy CDBs from medium-sized banks with an A rating. He accepts an interest rate of 7.5% per year, slightly higher than AAA, but still within a controlled risk profile.

These examples show how quality can be adjusted to your budget and objective, without needing to be an expert.

## Getting started 

If you still don't have clarity about the quality of your investments, follow these simple steps to start the journey.

- **Map your income**: note down all your earnings (salary, bonuses) and fixed expenses.
- **Define goals**: set a short-term goal (e.g., 6 months) and a long-term goal (e.g., retirement).
- **Choose high-quality assets**: look for products with an AAA or AA rating, such as CDBs from large banks or equity funds from consolidated companies.
- **Use a trusted app**: FinMoovi allows you to filter investments by rating, making it easy to choose high-quality assets with a few clicks.
- **Diversify**: combine high-quality fixed income with a small portion of moderate-risk assets to improve returns.

**Practical tip:** before buying, check the risk classification of the issuing institution; this avoids unpleasant surprises.

**Practical tip:** keep no more than 30% of your portfolio in higher-risk assets; this protects most of your capital.

**Practical tip:** review your allocation every six months and adjust according to changes in your income or quality indicators.
