## The everyday headache of hidden costs

Imagine you’re the owner of a small workshop that makes custom furniture. Every month you notice that the profit you thought you’d have disappears, but you can’t pinpoint why. The rent, the internet bill, the insurance policy – they all feel “necessary,” yet you never really see them in one place, and they silently eat into your cash flow. That vague, nagging feeling of “where did my money go?” is the classic pain of not knowing your **fixed expenses**.

![The everyday headache of hidden costs](/images/glossario/gasto-fixo-de-uma-empresa-inline-1.webp)



## What counts as a fixed expense?

A fixed expense is a cost that stays the same (or changes very predictably) every month, regardless of how much you produce or sell. It’s the financial equivalent of a subscription you can’t cancel without serious consequences.

- Rent or lease payments for the premises
- Utilities that have a base charge (electricity, water, internet)
- Salaries of permanent staff
- Insurance premiums
- Loan repayments
- Software subscriptions with a monthly fee
- Property taxes or municipal fees

These items appear on your profit‑and‑loss statement month after month, and they’re the first line of defense when you’re trying to understand why the bottom line looks thinner than expected.

## Defining the fixed expense for a company

In plain language, a **fixed expense** (sometimes called a fixed cost) is any recurring outflow that does not depend on production volume. Unlike variable costs—like raw material or shipping, which rise and fall with sales—fixed expenses stay steady. Because they’re predictable, they’re essential for budgeting, but they can also become a hidden drain if you don’t monitor them closely.

![Defining the fixed expense for a company](/images/glossario/gasto-fixo-de-uma-empresa-inline-3.webp)



## Why they matter for your bottom line

When you know exactly how much you spend on fixed items, you can:

- **Set realistic profit targets** – you’ll know the minimum revenue needed to cover those costs.
- **Identify negotiation opportunities** – a lease that’s been the same for years might be ripe for a discount.
- **Avoid cash‑flow surprises** – by planning for the inevitable, you won’t be caught off guard when a bill arrives.

Missing a single fixed expense can throw off your entire financial picture, especially for small businesses where every dollar counts.
