## What’s happening in your financial life right now?

You just got your paycheck and, as always, the first question that pops up is: “How am I going to make my money work?” The answer is usually “I’ll cut my daily coffee” or “I’ll postpone the dream trip.” But in practice, you end up swapping one thing for another without really knowing what you’re losing. That invisible trade‑off is what economists call **opportunity cost** – the value of what you give up when you choose an alternative.

![What’s happening in your financial life right now?](/images/glossario/custo-de-oportunidade-inline-1.webp)



Imagine you have 500 units of money available this month. You could use that amount to:

- Buy an online course package that could improve your career.  
- Save it for an emergency reserve.  
- Purchase that gadget you’ve been wanting for months.

If you decide to buy the gadget, the opportunity cost is the benefits the courses or the reserve would have brought. Without realizing it, you might be sacrificing a more secure future for an immediate pleasure.

## How opportunity cost affects everyday decisions

Most daily decisions have an embedded opportunity cost, even if we don’t notice it. Some common examples:

- **Time:** Watching a series for two hours could mean skipping a chapter of a book that offers insights for work.  
- **Money:** Spending the equivalent of one month’s rent on a fancy dinner prevents you from investing in a retirement fund.  
- **Space:** Keeping an old car ties up money in maintenance that could be used to pay the first installment of a property.

These choices are like a balance: what you put on one side leaves the other side empty. When the balance is off, the opportunity cost can turn into regret or missed chances.

## How to calculate opportunity cost in practice

1. **List the decision** – For example, buying a course that costs 300 units.  
2. **Identify the alternative** – Saving the same amount in an investment account that yields 5 % per year.  
3. **Calculate the alternative’s benefit** – 300 × 5 % ÷ 12 ≈ 1.25 units per month.  
4. **Compare** – If the course generates an income increase of 2 units per month, the opportunity cost is 1.25 - 2 = -0.75 (i.e., the course more than offsets what you’d have earned by investing).

FinMoovi does this calculation automatically when you set up extra‑income or investment goals, showing the “net gain” of each choice.

## When opportunity cost can be positive

Not every opportunity cost is negative. Sometimes giving something up brings unexpected benefits:

- **Free time:** Skipping overtime can improve your health, reducing future medical expenses.  
- **Experiences:** Spending on a trip can generate networking that opens professional doors, worth far more than the money spent.  
- **Education:** Investing in a course can accelerate your career, increasing income in future years.

The trick is to measure these benefits realistically, and FinMoovi helps by logging indirect gains (like salary raises or new opportunities) in the “extra income” categories.
