## What is Overdraft?

Overdraft is a [credit](/en/glossario/en-credit) limit that the bank makes available on your [checking account](/en/posts/en-digital-bank-vs-major-bank-which-is-worth-it-in-2026). When your balance goes negative, you automatically use the overdraft — and pay very high [interest](/en/glossario/en-interest) rates for it.

## Why is it so dangerous

- Interest rates of **8% to 15% per month** (150%+ per year)
- It is activated automatically (you don't even notice)
- Interest is charged daily on the negative balance
- Many people use it without realizing they are paying

## Practical example

If you are $200 overdrawn for 30 days with interest rates of 12% per month:
- Interest: $24
- In 6 months without paying: $200 becomes $395
- In 12 months: $200 becomes $780

## How to avoid?

1. **Disable the overdraft** — call the bank and ask to remove it  
2. **Keep a reserve in the account** — at least $100 as a cushion  
3. **Set up alerts** — warning when the balance is low  
4. **If you need credit** — a personal [loan](/en/glossario/en-loan) has interest rates 5 times lower
