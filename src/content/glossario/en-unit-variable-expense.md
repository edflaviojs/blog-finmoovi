## What a unit variable expense really looks like in everyday life  
Imagine you buy a coffee every morning, a snack at lunch, and a ride‑share to the office. Each of those items costs a few dollars, but add them up for a month and they eat up about one month’s rent. That “per‑item” cost that changes with how often you use it is what accountants call a **unit variable expense** – a spending item whose price varies each time you purchase it, unlike a fixed bill such as rent or insurance.

## Why it sneaks into your budget and hurts your goals  
When you think about budgeting, you usually list the big, predictable numbers: mortgage, utilities, internet. The tiny, fluctuating purchases don’t get the same attention, so they slip through the cracks. Over a year, those small, variable costs can add up to a sizable chunk of your cash flow, making it harder to hit savings targets or pay off debt.  

![What a unit variable expense really looks like in everyday life  ](/images/glossario/gasto-variavel-unitario-inline-1.webp)



- Coffee, snacks, and rides are **unit variable expenses** because you decide each day whether to spend them.  
- Their total amount depends on frequency, not on a set contract.  
- Because they’re irregular, they’re easy to overlook in a spreadsheet or a mental tally.  

The pain point is clear: you feel you’re “just spending a little,” yet the cumulative effect is a budget hole you can’t see until the month ends.
