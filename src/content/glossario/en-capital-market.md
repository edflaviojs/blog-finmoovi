## What is the capital market?

Imagine you receive a paycheck and, instead of letting the money sit idle, you want it to grow faster than a regular savings account. The place where companies, governments, and even you can raise or invest money over the long term is called the capital market. In simple terms, it’s a big marketplace where **long‑term financial assets**—like stocks (ownership shares) and bonds (loans to entities)—are bought and sold.  

![What is the capital market?](/images/glossario/mercado-de-capitais-inline-1.webp)



- **Stocks** give you a slice of a company’s profits and voting rights.  
- **Bonds** are like IOUs: you lend money and receive periodic interest plus the principal back later.  
- **Mutual funds and ETFs** pool many investors’ money to buy a diversified basket of stocks or bonds, making it easier for a regular person to participate.

The capital market is different from the everyday “cash market” where you pay for groceries or rent. It works on a longer horizon—months, years, even decades—so the returns can be higher, but the risk is also bigger. Understanding it helps you decide whether to keep money in a low‑interest account or send it to a place that can potentially fund your future goals, like buying a house, traveling, or retiring comfortably.

## How it works for you

When you hear “invest in the capital market,” you might picture a Wall Street trader shouting on a screen. In reality, you can start with just a few hundred dollars (or the equivalent of a few weeks’ rent) using a smartphone app. Here’s the basic flow:

1. **Choose an asset** – decide if you want a share of a company, a government bond, or a fund that mixes both.  
2. **Open a brokerage account** – this is the digital “wallet” that lets you buy and sell.  
3. **Deposit money** – transfer cash from your checking account; many apps let you do it instantly.  
4. **Place an order** – tell the platform how many units you want; the order is matched with a seller.  
5. **Hold or sell later** – you can keep the asset to collect dividends or interest, or sell when you need cash.

The key is **time**. The longer you stay invested, the more you benefit from compounding—earning returns on your returns. But you also need to watch fees, taxes, and market fluctuations. That’s where a good personal finance app can keep you on track.

## Why it matters

Most people think of investing as something only the wealthy can do. The truth is, the capital market is a tool for anyone who wants to protect their purchasing power against inflation (the gradual rise in prices). If a cup of coffee costs $3 today, it might cost $4 in a few years. Keeping all your money under the mattress or in a low‑interest account means you’re actually losing buying power.  

![Why it matters](/images/glossario/mercado-de-capitais-inline-3.webp)



Investing in stocks or diversified funds historically yields returns that outpace inflation, turning today’s modest savings into tomorrow’s larger nest egg. Even a small, regular contribution—say, the amount you spend on a weekend take‑out—can add up dramatically over 10, 20, or 30 years thanks to compound growth.

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Chasing quick gains**: The capital market isn’t a lottery. Avoid “hot tips” and focus on diversified, long‑term assets.  
- **Ignoring fees**: Even a 0.5 % annual fee can erode returns over decades. FinMoovi highlights the cheapest funds for each goal.  
- **Not rebalancing**: Over time, stocks may grow faster than bonds, skewing your risk profile. The app sends a reminder to rebalance annually, keeping your portfolio aligned with your comfort level.
