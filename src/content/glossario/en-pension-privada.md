## What is Private Pension?

Private Pension is a long‑term investment intended for the [jubilación](/es/posts/es-prevision-privada-2026-vale-la-pena-guia-practica). You contribute monthly and, in the future, withdraw the [valor](/es/glossario/es-valor-financiero) accumulated (with returns). It is a complement to the INSS retirement.

## PGBL vs. VGBL

| | PGBL | VGBL |
|--|------|------|
| Deduct IR? | Yes (up to 12% of income) | No |
| IR at redemption | On the total value | Only on the returns |
| Who is it for? | Files full declaration | Files simplified declaration |

## Tax tables

### Progressive (better for small withdrawals)

- Same table as salary (0% to 27.5%)

### Regressive (better for long term)

| Term | Rate |
|-------|----------|
| Up to 2 years | 35% |
| 2 to 4 years | 30% |
| 4 to 6 years | 25% |
| 6 to 8 years | 20% |
| 8 to 10 years | 15% |
| More than 10 years | 10% |

## Advantages

- Tax benefit (PGBL)
- Not included in estate inventory ([planificación](/es/posts/es-orcamento-com-salario-minimo-dicas-praticas) succession)
- Monthly investment discipline
- 10% rate after 10 years (regressive)

## Disadvantages

- High administration fees (many [fondos](/es/glossario/es-fondo-de-inversion) charge 1‑2%)
- Load [Tarifa](/es/glossario/es-tasa-financiera) (some charge on entry)
- The [rendimiento](/es/posts/es-montando-cartera-diversificada-con-r-500) may be lower than investing on your own
- Little flexibility

## Advice

If you are going to set up a pension, choose funds with administration fees below 1% and no load fee. Traditional banks tend to charge high fees — prefer independent brokerages.
