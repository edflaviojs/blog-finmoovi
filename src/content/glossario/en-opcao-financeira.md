## What is

An option is a contract that gives the buyer the right – but not the obligation – to buy or sell an asset (such as [stocks](/glossario/acoes), commodities or currencies) at a pre‑defined price, called the strike price, until an expiration date. There are two main categories: **call**, which gives the right to buy, and **put**, which gives the right to sell. Think of an option like insurance: you pay a small [value](/glossario/valor-financeiro) (premium) to ensure that, if the market moves against you, you can exercise the contract and limit the loss.

![O que é](/images/glossario/opcao-inline-1.webp)

## How it works

When you acquire an option, you pay a premium to the seller (also called the writer). If the asset’s price rises above the strike price of a call, you can exercise the option and buy the asset cheaper, profiting from the difference. If the price does not exceed the strike, the option expires worthless and you lose only the premium paid. The same applies to puts, but in reverse: they increase in value when the asset’s price falls.

- **Step 1:** Choose the asset you want to trade (e.g., Petrobras stocks).  
- **Step 2:** Define the type of option (call or put) and the strike price.  
- **Step 3:** Decide the expiration date (can be days, weeks or months).  
- **Step 4:** Pay the premium and wait for market movement.  
- **Step 5:** At expiration, decide whether to exercise the option or sell it on the secondary market.

## Advantages

Options provide flexibility and [leverage](/glossario/alavancagem), allowing you to participate in large price movements with an initial investment far smaller than what’s needed to buy the asset outright. They also serve as a portfolio protection tool, reducing risk during periods of high [volatility](/glossario/volatilidade).

![Vantagens](/images/glossario/opcao-inline-3.webp)

- **Leverage:** with a R$ 500 premium you can control R$ 20,000 in stocks.  
- **Protection:** an investor who holds stocks can buy puts to limit losses if the price falls.  
- **[Diversification](/glossario/diversificacao):** allows you to build strategies that combine different market scenarios, such as spreads and straddles.  
- **[Liquidity](/glossario/liquidez):** stock options of Brazilian companies usually have good trading on exchanges.

## Risks

Despite the advantages, options carry risks that cannot be ignored. The main risk is the total loss of the premium paid, which can happen if the option expires out‑of‑the‑money. Moreover, more advanced strategies can generate unlimited losses, especially when you sell uncovered options.

- **Total premium loss:** if the stock doesn’t reach the strike price, the invested money disappears.  
- **Complexity:** understanding the Greeks of options (Delta, Gamma, Theta) requires study and practice.  
- **Expiration:** unlike stocks, options have a limited term; time running against you can reduce the option’s value (the “theta” effect).  
- **Excessive leverage:** can amplify losses if the market moves against your position.

## Practical examples

Imagine you earn R$ 5,000 per month and have R$ 20,000 available to invest. You believe Vale’s shares (VALE3) will rise over the next three months. Today the stock is at R$ 70, and you buy a call with a strike price of R$ 75, expiring in 90 days, paying a premium of R$ 2.00 per share (R$ 200 for 100 shares).  

- **Optimistic scenario:** VALE3 rises to R$ 85. You exercise the option, buy the stock at R$ 75 and immediately sell at R$ 85, earning R$ 10 per share. The gross gain is R$ 1,000, minus the R$ 200 premium, resulting in R$ 800 profit.  
- **Neutral scenario:** VALE3 stays at R$ 73. The option expires worthless and you lose only the R$ 200 paid.  
- **Pessimistic scenario:** VALE3 falls to R$ 60. Even if you don’t exercise, the loss remains the R$ 200 premium.

Another example: you have a salary of R$ 8,000 and hold Ambev (ABEV3) shares valued at R$ 30,000. To protect against a drop, you buy puts with a strike price of R$ 28, paying R$ 1.50 per share (R$ 300 for 200 shares). If the stock falls to R$ 24, the put allows you to sell at R$ 28, limiting the loss to R$ 4 per share instead of R$ 6, plus the premium cost.

## How to start

If you’ve never traded options, the best approach is to start slowly, using only a small portion of your capital. First, open an account with a broker that offers a derivatives trading platform – many of them have intuitive apps, such as **Rico** or **Clear**, which work like a friend helping you understand each step. Then, follow these steps:

- **Study the basics:** read articles, watch videos and practice with simulators before putting real money.  
- **Define the goal:** protect your portfolio, seek leverage or just learn to trade? Your goal will determine the strategy.  
- **Safe allocation:** **Practical tip:** allocate at most 5 % of your total wealth to options until you gain confidence.  
- **Choose simple options:** start with short‑term calls or puts with strike prices close to the current price (at‑the‑money).  
- **Monitor time:** **Practical tip:** always check the “theta” of your option; the closer to expiration, the greater the value erosion.  
- **Record everything:** keep a trade journal, noting the reason for the trade, strike price, premium paid and outcome.
