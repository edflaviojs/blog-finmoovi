## What is Financial Education?

Financial education is learning to deal with [money](/posts/cashback-inteligente) intelligently. It's not about getting rich quick — it's about understanding how money works to make better decisions in everyday life.

## The 4 pillars

### 1. Earn

- Develop skills that the market values
- Negotiate salary
- Create extra income sources
- Entrepreneurial ventures

### 2. Spend

- Distinguish needs from wants
- Research before buying
- Avoid impulse purchases
- Use the 48‑hour rule (wait before buying)

### 3. Save

- Pay yourself first (set aside before spending)
- Automate transfers to [investments](/posts/cdb-vs-tesouro-selic-qual-rende-mais-em-2026)
- Have clear [savings](/posts/5-dicas-para-se-preparar-financeiramente-para-o-segundo-seme) goals
- [Emergency fund](/glossario/reserva-de-emergencia) as a priority

### 4. Invest

- Understand risk vs. return
- Start early ([compound interest](/glossario/juros-compostos))
- Diversify
- Be patient (long term)

## Why schools don't teach it

Unfortunately, financial education is not a priority in the Brazilian school curriculum. Most people learn about money through trial and error — usually after making costly mistakes.

## How to start

1. Record all your expenses for 30 days
2. Build a simple budget
3. Pay off expensive debts ([overdraft](/glossario/cheque-especial), revolving)
4. Build an emergency fund
5. Start investing (even R$ 50/month)
6. Read 1 finance book per quarter
7. Use FinMoovi to automate control
