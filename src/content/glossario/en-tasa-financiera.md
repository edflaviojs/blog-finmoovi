## What is it

The **taxa** is simply the rate or percentage applied to a financial operation, such as a loan, investment, or credit card. In everyday terms, it's the "cost" you pay for using someone else's money or the "gain" you get from lending yours. There's nothing mysterious behind it; it's like the price of a movie ticket, but for money. For example, if a bank offers you a rate of 12% per year for a loan, that means that for every R$ 1,000 you borrow, you'll pay R$ 120 in interest after one year.

![What is it](/images/glossario/taxa-inline-1.webp)



## How it works

The way the taxa is calculated depends on the type of product and term. In practice, the calculation is made by multiplying the capital (the amount borrowed or invested) by the rate and by the time the operation lasts. If the rate is **annual** and the term is 6 months, the rate is divided by 2.

- **Nominal Rate:** it's the one announced, without adjusting for the frequency of payments.
- **Effective Rate:** incorporates the periodicity (monthly, daily) and shows the real cost.

Suppose you earn R$ 4,500 per month and decide to invest R$ 2,000 in a fund that pays 8% per year. In 12 months, you'll receive approximately R$ 160 in interest (2,000 x 0.08). If the same fund pays 0.65% per month, the effective annual rate will be higher, around 8.3%, because the interest is capitalized every month.

## Advantages

- **[Savings](/es/posts/es-ahorra-en-las-compras-del-supermercado) in costs:** Knowing the taxa allows you to compare offers and choose the one with the lowest total cost.
- **Higher returns:** If you invest in products with a high and safe taxa, you can make your money work better than leaving it in your checking account.
- **Flexibility:** Some taxas are variable, allowing you to benefit if market conditions improve.

![Advantages](/images/glossario/taxa-inline-3.webp)



By understanding the taxa, you can negotiate better conditions with your bank or look for [alternatives](/es/posts/es-5-alternativas-a-organizze-en-2026-opciones-para-administrar) in fintechs (financial technology companies) that offer more competitive taxas. For example, a financial management app like **FinMoovi** shows you in real-time the taxa of your cards and loans, making it easier to compare.

## Risks

- **Variability:** Variable taxas can rise unexpectedly, increasing your payments.
- **Hidden costs:** Sometimes commissions or insurance are included that increase the total cost without the taxa seeming high.
- **Debt:** An attractive taxa can tempt you to ask for more credit than you really need, generating a difficult financial burden to manage.

If your salary is R$ 6,000 and you take a loan of R$ 10,000 with a taxa of 18% per year, the monthly payment will be approximately R$ 300. If the taxa rises to 22% due to a variable condition, the payment increases to R$ 350, affecting your [budget](/es/posts/es-5-alternativas-al-mobills-en-2026) monthly.

## Practical examples

Imagine you receive R$ 5,000 per month and want to buy a motorcycle for R$ 20,000. You have two options:

- **[Financing](/es/glossario/es-financiamiento) traditional:** taxa of 15% per year, term 48 months.
- **Financing in a fintech:** taxa of 11% per year, term 48 months, without commissions.

With the first option, you would pay around R$ 560 per month; with the second, R$ 500. The difference of R$ 60 per month translates to R$ 2,880 at the end of the contract, which you could save or invest.

Another case: you decide to invest R$ 3,000 in a [CDB](/es/glossario/es-cdb) (Certificate of Bank Deposit) that pays 7% per year. After one year, you'll have R$ 3,210. If instead, you leave it in your checking account with a taxa of 0.5% per year, you'll earn only R$ 15. The difference is clear: the higher taxa gives you higher returns.

## How to start

1. **Review your account statements:** Identify the taxas you already pay (cards, loans).
2. **Compare offers:** Use apps or comparison sites to find the lowest taxa for the product you need.
3. **Calculate the total cost:** Don't just look at the announced taxa; add commissions, insurance, and other charges.

**Practical tip:** Write down all the taxas you pay and the ones you could get in a sheet or in your financial app; you'll quickly see where you can save.

**Practical tip:** If you're going to ask for a loan, choose the shortest term you can pay without compromising your essential expenses; a higher taxa for a short term is usually less expensive than a low taxa for many years.

**Practical tip:** Automate your debt payments before the due date; this way, you avoid late fees that increase the effective taxa.

Once you have a clear idea of which taxa is most convenient for you, open an account with the chosen institution (traditional bank or fintech) and set up automatic payments. Discipline is key to making the taxa work in your favor and not against you.
