## What is Profit Margin?

Profit margin is how much is left from each real that comes in. If a company invoices R$ 100 and has R$ 80 of [costs](/es/posts/es-prevision-privada-2026-vale-la-pena-guia-practica), its profit margin is 20%. The higher the margin, the more efficient the business.

## Types of margin

### Gross Margin

- (Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold) ÷ Revenue × 100
- Shows efficiency in production/purchasing

### Operating Margin

- (Revenue - Costs - Operating Expenses) ÷ Revenue × 100
- Shows operational efficiency

### Net Margin

- Net Profit ÷ Revenue × 100
- Shows how much actually remains at the end

## Practical example (clothing store)

| | [Value](/es/glossario/es-valor-financiero) | Margin |
|--|-------|--------|
| Revenue | R$ 50.000 | - |
| Cost of clothing | R$ 20.000 | Gross: 60% |
| Expenses (rent, salaries) | R$ 20.000 | Operating: 20% |
| Taxes | R$ 5.000 | Net: 10% |
| **Net profit** | **R$ 5.000** | |

## What it's used for (investor)

When analyzing [stocks](/es/glossario/es-acoes), profit margin shows:
- Companies with high margins have a competitive advantage
- Growing margins indicate improved efficiency
- Very low margins indicate a fragile business

## For [personal finance](/es/posts/es-5-alternativas-a-organizze-en-2026-opciones-para-administrar)

You also have a “margin”: how much is left from your salary after all the [expenses](/es/posts/es-5-consejos-para-prepararse-financieramente-para-el-segundo-s). If you earn R$ 5.000 and R$ 1.000 remains, your personal margin is 20%. Ideally at least 20% (50-30-20 rule).
