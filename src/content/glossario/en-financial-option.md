## What it is

An option is a contract that gives the buyer the right – but not the obligation – to buy or sell an asset (stock, index, [currency](/en/glossario/en-moeda) etc.) at a predefined price, called the strike price, up to a deadline. In simple terms, it’s like reserving a price today to use later, if the situation is favorable.  

![What it is](/images/glossario/opcao-inline-1.webp)

- **Right, not obligation:** you can decide to exercise or let the option expire.  
- **Strike price:** fixed [value](/en/glossario/en-financial-value) that will be used to buy or sell the asset.  
- **Expiration date:** last day the option can be exercised.  
- **Premium:** amount paid to the option seller to secure this right.

## How it works

When you buy an option, you pay the premium and receive the right to operate in the future. If the asset price rises (in the case of a call option) or falls (in the case of a put option) beyond the strike price, you can exercise the option and profit; if not, you only lose the premium.  

- **Step 1:** choose the asset you want to protect or speculate.  
- **Step 2:** set the strike price and the expiration date.  
- **Step 3:** pay the premium to the option seller.  
- **Step 4:** monitor the market until expiration.  
- **Step 5:** decide whether to exercise or let the option expire.

## Advantages

Options bring flexibility and can be used both to protect investments and to seek higher gains with little capital.  

![Advantages](/images/glossario/opcao-inline-3.webp)

- **[Leverage](/en/glossario/en-leverage):** with a small premium you control a larger amount of assets.  
- **Protection ([hedge](/en/glossario/en-hedge)):** allows limiting losses in a portfolio of [stocks](/en/glossario/en-stocks).  
- **Extra income:** by selling options, you receive the premium as additional income.  
- **[Diversification](/en/glossario/en-diversification):** opens possibilities of strategies that do not exist in traditional investments.

## Risks

Despite the advantages, options carry risks that cannot be ignored.  

- **Total loss of premium:** if the option expires out of the money, you lose the amount paid.  
- **Complexity:** understanding the Greeks of options (Delta, Theta, etc.) requires study.  
- **Volatility:** sharp movements can make the strategy unfavorable quickly.  
- **Liquidity:** not all options have enough volume to buy or sell without impacting the price.

## Practical examples

Imagine you earn **$1,000 per month** and have **$6,000 invested** in a stock of a large mining company. You fear that, over the next three months, the stock could fall due to market instability.  

- **Example 1 – Protection (put):** you buy a put option with a strike price of **$18**, paying a premium of **$0.40 per share**. Each contract covers 100 shares, so the total cost is **$40**. If the stock falls to $16, you exercise the option and sell at $18, limiting the loss. If the stock stays above $18, you lose only the $40.  
- **Example 2 – Leverage (call):** you believe a tech company’s stock will rise in the next two months. You buy a call option with a strike price of **$3**, paying **$0.30 per share**. Investing **$60** (200 contracts), you control 20,000 shares. If the price rises to $4, the profit per share is $1, minus the premium, generating a significant gain. If it doesn’t rise, you lose the $60.  
- **Example 3 – Extra income (sell call):** you own 200 shares of an energy company that are worth **$6 each**. You sell a call option with a strike price of **$7**, receiving **$0.20 per share** (total **$40**). If the stock does not exceed $7, you keep the premium and still hold the shares. If it rises above $7, you will have to sell the shares at that price, but you still profit thanks to the premium.

## How to start

Starting to trade options doesn’t have to be complicated, just follow a few steps and stay disciplined.  

- **Open an account with a broker:** choose one that offers a user‑friendly platform and support in English.  
- **Study the basics:** understand what call, put, strike price, expiration and premium are.  
- **Practice with a simulator:** many brokers have demo accounts that allow trading without risk.  
- **Define your goal:** protection, speculation or income generation? This guides the choice of strategy.  
- **Start small:** use only 5 % to 10 % of your investment capital for the first trades.  
- **Follow the market:** keep an eye on news that affect the assets you chose.  

**Practical tip:** **Always write down the reason for your trade and the entry price; this helps avoid impulsive decisions.**  
**Practical tip:** **Use a stop‑loss or limit on the premium paid; this way you control the maximum loss.**  
**Practical tip:** **Reevaluate the strategy at each expiration; if it didn’t work, adjust the strike price or the term.**
