## What are LCI and LCA?

LCI (Letra de [Crédito](/es/posts/es-tarjeta-de-credito-vs-tarjeta-de-debito-cual-vale-mas-la-pen) Inmobiliario) and LCA (Letra de Crédito del Agronegocio) are [renta fixa](/es/glossario/es-renda-fixa) investments issued by banks. The big advantage: they are **exentas de [Impuesto a la Renta](/es/glossario/es-imposto-de-renda)** for individuals.

## Difference between LCI and LCA

The only difference is the purpose of the money:
- **LCI:** the bank uses it to finance the real‑estate sector
- **LCA:** the bank uses it to finance agribusiness

For the investor, they work the same way.

## Advantages

- Tax exemption (higher net return)
- FGC protection up to R$ 250.000
- Return usually linked to the [CDI](/es/glossario/es-cdi) (85‑100%)
- Security similar to [CDB](/es/glossario/es-cdb)

## Disadvantages

- Minimum lock‑up of 90 days (cannot be redeemed earlier)
- Minimum investment usually higher (R$ 1.000‑5.000)
- Fewer options available than CDB

## LCI/LCA vs. CDB

An LCI that pays 85% of CDI can yield more than a CDB at 100% of CDI, because of the tax exemption:

| | CDB 100% CDI | LCI 85% CDI |
|--|-------------|-------------|
| Gross return | 13,65% | 11,60% |
| IR (15%) | -2,05% | 0% |
| **Net return** | **11,60%** | **11,60%** |

In this example, they earn the same. But an LCI at 90% of CDI already outperforms a CDB at 100%.

## When to invest in LCI/LCA

- When you won’t need the money for at least 90 days
- To diversify the [renta fixa](/es/posts/es-cdb-vs-tesouro-selic-cual-rinde-mas-en-2026)
- When you find rates above 90% of CDI
