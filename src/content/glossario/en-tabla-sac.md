## What is the SAC table?

SAC (Constant [Amortization](/es/glossario/es-amortizacion) System) is a financing repayment method where the [value](/es/glossario/es-valor-financiero) of the amortization (the part that reduces the debt) is the same in all installments. Since the [interest](/es/glossario/es-juros) decreases as the outstanding balance falls, the installments become smaller over time.

## How does it work?

- **Amortization:** fixed (loan amount ÷ number of installments)
- **Interest:** calculated on the outstanding balance (decreases each month)
- **Installment:** amortization + interest (starts high, ends low)

## Practical example

[Financing](/es/glossario/es-financiamiento) of R$ 120,000 over 10 years (120 months) at 1% per month:
- Monthly amortization: R$ 120,000 ÷ 120 = R$ 1,000
- 1st installment: R$ 1,000 + R$ 1,200 (interest) = **R$ 2,200**
- 60th installment: R$ 1,000 + R$ 610 = **R$ 1,610**
- 120th installment: R$ 1,000 + R$ 10 = **R$ 1,010**

## SAC vs. Price

| | SAC | Price |
|--|-----|-------|
| Installments | Decreasing | Fixed |
| Total interest | Lower | Higher |
| First installment | Higher | Lower |
| Ideal for | Those who can pay more at the start | Those who need a fixed installment |

## When to choose SAC

- When your income allows you to pay higher installments at the beginning
- When you want to save on total interest
- In long‑term financing (the difference is huge)
- When you plan to prepay the loan
