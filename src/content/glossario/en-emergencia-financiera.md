## What is a Financial Emergency?

A financial emergency is any unexpected situation that requires money you hadn't planned to spend. It can be a layoff, a health issue, a car repair, or a broken appliance.

## Common examples

- Job loss (requires 3-6 months of [expenses](/es/posts/es-5-consejos-para-prepararse-financieramente-para-el-segundo-s))
- Medical emergency (surgery, treatment)
- Car repair (R$ 2.000-5.000)
- Broken appliance (refrigerator, washing machine)
- Housing problem (leak, pipe)
- Unexpected fine or tax

## How to prepare

The best protection against financial emergencies is the **emergency reserve**:
- 6 months of expenses for CLT
- 12 months for freelancers/Legal Entities
- Invested with daily [liquidity](/es/glossario/es-liquidez) ([Tesouro Selic](/es/posts/es-cdb-vs-tesouro-selic-cual-rinde-mas-en-2026), [CDB](/es/glossario/es-cdb))

## What to do if you don’t have a reserve

If the emergency hits and you don’t have a reserve:

1. **Don’t use overdraft** ([interest](/es/glossario/es-juros) of 15%/month)
2. **Don’t use the credit card revolving** (interest of 16%/month)
3. Apply for a payroll loan (interest of 1-2%/month)
4. Anticipate FGTS or the 13th salary
5. Sell something you don’t use (marketplace)
6. Negotiate a direct payment plan with the creditor

## Prevention

After resolving the emergency, prioritize building your emergency reserve. Start with R$ 1.000 (covers most small unforeseen expenses) and increase it up to 6 months of expenses.
