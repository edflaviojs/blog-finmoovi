## How Nasdaq works in plain language

- **Exchange** – A place (physical or digital) where buyers and sellers meet. Nasdaq is electronic, so trades happen in milliseconds.
- **Index** – A statistical measure that tracks a group of stocks. The Nasdaq Composite includes over 3,000 companies; the Nasdaq‑100 focuses on the 100 biggest non‑financial firms.
- **Ticker symbol** – A short code that identifies a company (e.g., AAPL for Apple). When you see “AAPL +3%,” it means Apple’s share price rose 3% today.

Think of the Nasdaq like a giant farmer’s market. Each stall (company) sells a product (shares). The overall vibe of the market—whether it’s bustling or quiet—gets captured by the index. If most stalls are selling popular items, the market’s “price level” goes up, and the index reflects that.

## Why the Nasdaq matters for everyday investors

- **Tech focus** – Because it lists many technology firms, a rise often signals confidence in innovation, which can translate into higher returns over time.
- **Liquidity** – Lots of buyers and sellers mean you can usually buy or sell without huge price swings.
- **Transparency** – Prices are updated in real time, so you always know the current value of your investment.
