## What is Financial Education?

Financial education is learning to manage [money](/es/posts/es-ahorrando-en-la-cuenta-de-energia) intelligently. It's not about getting rich quickly — it's about understanding how money works to make better everyday decisions.

## The 4 pillars

### 1. Earn

- Develop skills that the market values
- Negotiate salary
- Create extra income sources
- Start a business

### 2. Spend

- Differentiate needs from wants
- Research before buying
- Avoid impulse purchases
- Use the 48‑hour rule (wait before buying)

### 3. Save

- Pay yourself first (set aside before spending)
- Automate transfers for investments
- Have clear [saving](/es/posts/es-ahorra-en-las-compras-del-supermercado) goals
- Emergency reserve as a priority

### 4. Invest

- Understand risk vs. return
- Start early (compound interest)
- Diversify
- Be patient (long term)

## Why schools don't teach it

Unfortunately, financial education is not a priority in the Brazilian school curriculum. Most people learn about money by trial and error — usually after making costly mistakes.

## How to start

1. Track all your [expenses](/es/posts/es-5-consejos-para-prepararse-financieramente-para-el-segundo-s) for 30 days
2. Build a simple [budget](/es/posts/es-5-consejos-para-reorganizar-tus-finanzas-en-vacaciones)
3. Pay off expensive debts (overdraft, credit card revolving)
4. Create an emergency reserve
5. Start investing (even R$ 50/month)
6. Read 1 finance book per quarter
7. Use [FinMoovi](/es/posts/es-5-alternativas-al-mobills-en-2026) to automate control
