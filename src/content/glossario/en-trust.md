## What is a Trust

Imagine you’re buying a house and you want to make sure the money you’ve saved doesn’t get tangled up in probate, taxes, or family disputes after you’re gone. You’ve heard the word “trust” tossed around, but you’re not sure if it’s a fancy legal tool you can’t afford or a practical way to protect what you’ve worked for. A trust is simply a legal arrangement where you (the grantor) transfer assets to a trustee, who manages them for the benefit of your chosen beneficiaries. Think of it as handing the keys of a safe to someone you trust—literally—to open it only when the conditions you set are met.

![What is a Trust](/images/glossario/trust-inline-1.webp)



A trust can hold anything from cash, stocks, and real‑estate to a vintage car or a collection of art. The key advantage is control: you decide who gets what, when, and under which circumstances. Unlike a will, which only takes effect after death and must go through probate (a public, often lengthy court process), a trust can distribute assets instantly, keep details private, and sometimes reduce taxes.

## Why People Usually Need a Trust

- **Protecting minor children** – You can set age milestones or education goals before they receive any money.
- **Avoiding probate** – Assets move directly to beneficiaries without court delays.
- **Managing disability** – If you become unable to handle finances, the trustee steps in automatically.
- **Preserving family businesses** – A trust can keep ownership within the family while providing professional management.
- **Charitable giving** – You can earmark a portion of your wealth for a cause you care about, with tax benefits.

Most people think a trust is only for the ultra‑rich, but even a modest portfolio—say, the equivalent of a few months’ rent, a modest investment account, and a car—can benefit from the structure if you want clear rules for the future.

## Practical Steps to Build Your First Trust

**Practical tip:** **Start small.** Transfer a single, low‑value asset—like the cash equivalent of a few weeks’ groceries—into a “starter trust” to get comfortable with the process.

1. **Define the purpose** – Write down why you need the trust (e.g., “provide for my child’s education”).
2. **Choose a trustee** – This can be a trusted family member, a friend, or a professional service. Make sure they understand the responsibility.
3. **List the assets** – Use FinMoovi’s smart capture to photograph deeds, bank statements, or even a photo of a valuable watch. The app will auto‑categorize them.
4. **Set distribution rules** – Specify ages, milestones, or conditions (e.g., “release 25 % when the beneficiary turns 25, the rest at 30”).
5. **Document everything** – FinMoovi generates a PDF summary of all assets, values, and rules that you can attach to your legal trust document.

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

- **Skipping the trustee interview** – Even if you pick a close friend, make sure they’re comfortable with bookkeeping. FinMoovi’s shared view lets them see the trust’s balance without exposing your personal spending.
- **Forgetting tax implications** – Some trusts trigger annual filing requirements. Use the app’s “tax estimate” widget to get a ballpark figure and talk to a professional if it looks high.
- **Overcomplicating the rules** – The more conditions you add, the harder it is to manage. Keep it simple: a few clear milestones are easier to track in FinMoovi’s timeline view.

**Practical tip:** **Review the trust annually** – Open FinMoovi, glance at the cash‑flow report, and adjust contributions or distributions as life changes.
