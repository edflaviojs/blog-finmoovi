## What it is

A **option** is a contract that gives you the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an asset (such as stocks, currencies, or commodities) at a predetermined price and within a set time frame. Think of it as a price reservation: you pay a small amount today to ensure you can buy or sell later at the price you agreed on. In the world of [finanzas personales](/es/posts/es-5-alternativas-a-organizze-en-2026-opciones-para-administrar), options can be used to protect your [inversiones](/es/posts/es-cdb-vs-tesouro-selic-cual-rinde-mas-en-2026) or to try to earn extra gains without committing all your capital.

![Qué es](/images/glossario/opcao-inline-1.webp)



## How it works

When you acquire an option, you pay a premium (the price of the option). That premium is the only money you lose if you decide not to exercise the contract. There are two basic types:

- **Call (buy option):** allows you to buy the asset at a fixed price (strike price) before the option expires.
- **Put (sell option):** allows you to sell the asset at the strike price within the same time frame.

The typical process is summarized in three steps:

- **Select the asset** (for example, shares of a Brazilian company).
- **Choose the type of option** (call or put) and the strike price that seems reasonable to you.
- **Pay the premium** and wait for the market to move. If the asset’s price rises (for a call) or falls below (for a put) the strike price, you can exercise the option and make a profit; if not, you simply let the option expire and lose the premium.

## Advantages

Options offer several benefits that can be useful even for someone earning between R$3.000 and R$8.000 per month:

![Ventajas](/images/glossario/opcao-inline-3.webp)



- **Protection against drops:** a put can act like insurance for your stocks, limiting the maximum loss.
- **Moderate leverage:** with a small premium you can control a position much larger than the capital you invested.
- **Flexibility:** you can combine options (strategies like spreads) to tailor the risk to your profile.
- **Extra income:** selling call options on stocks you already own (the “covered call” strategy) can bring in monthly premiums.

## Risks

Like any financial instrument, options carry risks you should not underestimate:

- **Total loss of the premium:** if the option expires without [valor](/es/glossario/es-valor-financiero), the amount paid disappears.
- **Complexity:** understanding concepts like [volatilidad](/es/glossario/es-volatilidad), delta and expiration requires study; a mistake can cost more than expected.
- **Excessive leverage:** even though the premium is low, exposure to the underlying asset can be high, generating indirect losses.
- **[Liquidez](/es/glossario/es-liquidez):** not all options have enough market movement, making it hard to buy or sell at a good price.

## Practical examples

Suppose your monthly salary is R$5.000 and you have R$10.000 invested in Petrobras shares (PETR4). Here are three scenarios with options:

- **Protection with a put:** you buy a put option with a strike price of R$30 and a 3‑month expiration, paying a premium of R$200. If the stock falls to R$25, you exercise the option and sell at R$30, limiting your loss to the R$5 per share difference plus the R$200 premium.
- **Profit with a call:** you think the stock will rise and acquire a call at R$35, paying R$150. If at expiration the stock is at R$40, you exercise the option, buy at R$35 and immediately sell at R$40, earning a R$5 per share profit minus the premium.
- **Income with a covered call:** you own 200 Vale shares (VALE3) and sell a call with a strike price of R$100, receiving a premium of R$300. If the stock stays below R$100, you keep your shares and the premium adds to your monthly income.

In each case, the premium represents a fraction of the total capital (between 1% and 3% of the position’s value), allowing you to experiment without committing more than 5% of your savings.

## How to start

If you decide to try options, follow these steps so you don’t slip up:

- **Basic education:** devote at least one hour a week to reading articles or watching videos about options; platforms like YouTube and personal finance blogs are a good starting point.
- **Open an account with a broker:** choose one that offers a user‑friendly app (for example, the app of XP Investimentos) and that allows option trading without excessive fees.
- **Define your goal:** decide whether you’re looking for protection, extra income, or speculation; that will determine the type of option you’ll buy.
- **Start with a small premium:** don’t risk more than 2% of your net worth on the first trade; that way, if the option expires worthless, the loss is minimal.
- **Use a spreadsheet:** record each trade (asset, type, strike price, premium, expiration) to evaluate results and learn from mistakes.
- **Watch the expiration:** never leave an option unattended; if the market moves in your favor, act before it expires.

**Practical tip:** **Keep an [fondo de emergencia](/es/glossario/es-fondo-de-emergencia) separate before trading options**, so you avoid touching money meant for essential expenses.

**Practical tip:** **Don’t let emotions drive you**; if the stock spikes quickly, assess whether to exercise the option or sell it in the market to lock in gains.

**Practical tip:** **Check implied volatility**, which shows how much the market expects the price to vary; high volatility usually makes the premium more expensive, but it also raises the profit potential.
