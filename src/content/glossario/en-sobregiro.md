## What is Overdraft?

Overdraft is a [credit](/es/posts/es-tarjeta-de-credito-vs-tarjeta-de-debito-cual-vale-mas-la-pen) limit that the bank makes available in your checking account. When your balance goes negative, you automatically use the overdraft — and pay [interest](/es/glossario/es-juros) that is sky‑high.

## Why it’s so dangerous

- Interest rates of **8% to 15% per month** (150%+ per year)
- It activates automatically (you don’t even notice it)
- Interest is charged daily on the negative balance
- Many people use it without knowing they’re paying

## Practical example

If you end up R$ 1,000 negative for 30 days with a 12% monthly interest rate:
- Interest: R$ 120
- In 6 months without paying: R$ 1,000 becomes R$ 1,973
- In 12 months: R$ 1,000 becomes R$ 3,896

## How to avoid?

1. **Disable the overdraft** — call the bank and request its removal  
2. **Keep a reserve in the account** — at least R$ 500 as a cushion  
3. **Set up alerts** — notification when the balance is low  
4. **If you need [credit](/es/glossario/es-credito)** — personal loans have interest rates 5 times lower
