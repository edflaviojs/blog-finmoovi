## What is a Financial Goal?

A financial goal is a concrete objective for your [money](/posts/cashback-inteligente). It's not “I want to save more” — it's “I want to save R$ 10,000 by December 2025 to put down on a car”. The more specific it is, the higher the chance of achieving it.

## How to set good goals (SMART method)

- **S**pecific: “Save R$ 10,000” (not “save more”)
- **M**easurable: exact [value](/glossario/valor-financeiro) that can be tracked
- **A**chievable: possible with your current income
- **R**elevant: important to you
- **T**ime‑bound: with a defined deadline

## Goal examples

### Short‑term (up to 1 year)

- Build an [emergency reserve](/glossario/reserva-de-emergencia) of R$ 15,000
- Pay off [credit card](/posts/cartao-de-credito-vs-cartao-de-debito-qual-vale-mais-a-pena-) debt of R$ 3,000
- Save R$ 5,000 for a [trip](/posts/voce-perde-o-controle-dos-gastos-quando-viaja-usando-moedas-)

### Medium‑term (1‑5 years)

- Save R$ 50,000 for a down payment on an apartment
- Replace the car (R$ 30,000)
- Do an exchange program (R$ 20,000)

### Long‑term (5+ years)

- [Financial independence](/glossario/independencia-financeira) (R$ 1,500,000)
- Complementary retirement
- Buy a property outright

## How to track

1. Set the total amount and the deadline
2. Divide by the number of months (required monthly contribution)
3. Automate the monthly transfer
4. Track the progress (apps like FinMoovi help)
5. Celebrate intermediate milestones (25%, 50%, 75%)

## Tip

Keep a maximum of 3 simultaneous goals. Too many goals split your focus and money — none progress fast enough to stay motivating.
