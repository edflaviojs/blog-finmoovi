## What is Volatility?

Volatility is the intensity with which the price of an investment goes up and down. A volatile asset can rise 5 % in one day and fall 3 % the next day. The higher the volatility, the higher the risk — but also the higher the potential gain.

## Volatility examples

| Asset | Volatility | Typical daily variation |
|--------|-------------|--------------------------|
| [Poupança](/es/glossario/es-poupanca) | Almost zero | 0 % |
| [Tesouro Selic](/es/posts/es-cdb-vs-tesouro-selic-cual-rinde-mas-en-2026) | Very low | 0,03 % |

## Is volatility bad?

Not necessarily. It depends on your profile:
- **Conservative:** volatility is stress. Prefers [renda fixa](/es/glossario/es-renda-fixa).
- **Moderate:** accepts some fluctuation for higher return.
- **Aggressive:** uses volatility to their advantage (buy on dips).

## How to deal with volatility

1. **Have a long horizon** — short term, everything swings  
2. **Diversify** — different assets swing at different times  
3. **Don’t check every day** — checking prices daily creates anxiety  
4. **Invest regularly** — monthly contributions smooth the average price  
5. **Keep an emergency reserve** — so you don’t have to sell low  

## Volatility index

In Brazil, the main indicator is the **VIX** (fear index). When the VIX is high, the market is nervous. When it’s low, it’s calm.
