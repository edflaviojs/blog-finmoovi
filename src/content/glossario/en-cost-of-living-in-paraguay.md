## What is the cost of living in Paraguay

The phrase “custo de vida no Paraguai” is simply the amount of money you need each month to cover basic expenses—housing, food, transport, health, and a little leisure—while you’re living there. It’s not a fixed number; it changes with the city you choose, your lifestyle, and whether you earn in the local currency or abroad. Most people feel the pinch when they compare their paycheck with the price of a daily coffee or the rent of a modest apartment, and suddenly the budget feels tighter than expected.

## How it impacts your personal budget

When you know the real cost of living, you can decide if a job offer, a remote‑work contract, or a retirement plan is viable. Without that knowledge, you might end up spending more than you earn, borrowing to cover groceries, or missing out on savings opportunities. The impact shows up in three main ways:

- **Cash flow gaps:** Money leaves the account faster than it arrives, forcing you to dip into emergency funds.
- **Hidden inflation:** Small price increases (like a slightly higher bus fare) accumulate and erode purchasing power.
- **Opportunity cost:** Money tied up in everyday expenses can’t be invested in assets that grow over time.

## Estimating the real numbers

Start by breaking the budget into categories that reflect your daily life. A quick way to get a realistic picture is to list the most common expenses and assign a relative value—think “about one month’s rent” or “the price of a weekly grocery basket.” Then, add a buffer for unexpected costs.

![Estimating the real numbers](/images/glossario/custo-de-vida-no-paraguai-inline-3.webp)



- Housing: rent or mortgage, utilities, internet
- Food: groceries, occasional meals out, coffee
- Transport: public transit passes, fuel, occasional taxi
- Health: basic insurance, medicines, occasional doctor visit
- Leisure: streaming services, weekend outings, small trips

Once you have the list, total the amounts and compare them with your monthly income. If the sum is close to or exceeds what you earn, you need to adjust either the income side (maybe a side gig) or the expense side (cutting non‑essential items).

## Common mistakes people make

Many newcomers to Paraguay underestimate the cost of living because they rely on anecdotal prices or tourist‑focused information. The most frequent errors are:

- Ignoring seasonal price swings for fruits and vegetables, which can double the grocery bill in certain months.
- Forgetting that utilities (especially heating in colder regions) are billed after consumption, not included in the rent.
- Assuming that a “cheap” apartment automatically means lower overall expenses; older buildings often have higher water and electricity usage.
