## What is it
The term "investment fund" is commonly used in the financial context to refer to collective investments, where several people put their money in a diversified portfolio of assets, such as stocks, bonds, real estate, etc. These funds are managed by experienced professionals whose goal is to maximize returns for investors. Imagine you and a few dozen friends decide to pool money to invest in different types of assets, but instead of doing it alone, you hire a specialist to make the investment decisions. That's basically what a fund does, but on a much larger scale and with a much wider variety of investment options.

## How it works
A fund works basically as follows: investors put their money into the fund, and in return receive units or shares of the fund. The fund manager then uses the collected money to invest in a portfolio of assets, which can include everything from company stocks to debt securities, real estate, and much more. The goal is to diversify investments to minimize risks and maximize returns. For example, if you invest R$ 1.000 in an equity fund, the fund manager can use that money to buy shares of different companies, such as Vale, Petrobras, or [Nubank](/glossario/nubank). If the value of those shares rises, the value of the fund's units also rises, and you can sell your units for a higher price than you initially paid.

![What is](/images/glossario/fundos-inline-1.webp)

* Types of funds:
  * Equity funds: invest mainly in company stocks
  * Bond funds: invest in debt securities, such as government or corporate bonds
  * [Real estate funds](/glossario/fundos-imobiliarios): invest in properties or companies in the real estate sector
  * Multi‑market funds: invest in a combination of different asset types

## Advantages
Funds offer several advantages for investors. One of the main ones is **[diversification](/glossario/diversificacao)**, which helps reduce risk. When you invest in a fund, your money is spread across different assets, meaning if one asset underperforms, others can compensate. Additionally, funds are **managed by professionals**, who have experience and knowledge to make informed investment decisions. Another advantage is **[liquidity](/glossario/liquidez)**, as you can buy or sell fund units easily, depending on the type of fund. **Practical tip:** before investing in a fund, make sure to read the prospectus and understand the investment objectives, the risks, and the fees charged.

## Risks
Although funds offer several advantages, there are also risks involved. One of the main risks is **market risk**, which affects the value of the assets the fund invests in. If the stock market, for example, experiences a drop, the value of the fund's units can also fall. Another risk is **credit risk**, which occurs when the companies or entities the fund invests in have difficulty paying their debts. In addition, funds also charge **management fees**, which can affect the fund's performance. **Practical tip:** it is important to **diversify your investments** to minimize risks, and not put all your eggs in one basket.

## Practical examples
Let's consider a practical example. Imagine you earn R$ 5.000 per month and decide to invest 10% of your salary, or R$ 500, in an equity fund. If the fund has an average performance of 8% per year, after one year the value of your investment could be approximately R$ 5.400, considering [compound interest](/glossario/juros-compostos). **Practical tip:** **start early** to invest, as time is a great ally when it comes to long‑term investments. Also, it is important to **reinvest the [dividends](/glossario/dividendos)** to maximize returns.
